title: Do you need to file a tax return?
level: starter
summary: The 2026 rules for teens who are claimed as dependents, and why filing is often worth it even when you do not have to.

A **tax return** is the form you send the IRS once a year to report what you earned and figure your tax for the year. Whether you must file depends on how much you earned, what kind of income it was, and whether someone claims you as a dependent. Many teens do not have to file, but many of them should anyway.

## Are you a dependent?

You are a **dependent** if a parent or guardian can claim you on their tax return. Most teens who live at home and are supported by their family are dependents. Ask whoever files your family's taxes, because it changes your numbers and you will need to check a box about it on your return.

## Your standard deduction as a dependent

A dependent gets a smaller standard deduction than other single filers. For 2026, it is the greater of $1,350 or your earned income plus $450, but never more than the full $16,100. **Earned income** means pay from work: wages, tips, and self-employment earnings.

For a teen with only a job, that formula almost always covers every dollar earned, so the income tax owed is $0.

```ledger Nia's 2026 standard deduction
Earned income from her grocery job | $5,200.00
+ Add-on for dependents | $450.00
= Her standard deduction | $5,650.00
# $5,650 is more than $1,350 and less than $16,100, so it counts in full.
```

## When you must file

For 2026, a dependent must file a federal return in these common cases:

- Your earned income from a job is more than $16,100.
- You have $400 or more of net earnings from self-employment, such as gig work, lawn care, or tutoring. This is true even if you owe no income tax, because self-employment tax is figured on the return.

If you also have money from investments, such as interest, dividends, or a stock sale, different limits apply. The IRS [Do I need to file?](https://www.irs.gov/help/ita/do-i-need-to-file-a-tax-return) tool walks you through every case in a few minutes.

```table Do you need to file for 2026?
| Your situation | Must you file? | Should you file? |
| Only wages, $16,100 or less, nothing withheld | No | Optional |
| Only wages, $16,100 or less, income tax withheld | No | Yes, to get it back |
| Only wages, more than $16,100 | Yes | Yes |
| $400 or more of self-employment net earnings | Yes | Yes |
```

## Why file when you do not have to

If federal income tax was withheld from your paychecks and you owe none, the only way to get that money back is to file. Nobody sends it automatically. Check box 2 of your W-2: any amount there is federal income tax that was withheld.

```ledger Nia's refund if she files
Federal income tax withheld, W-2 box 2 | $64.00
- Federal income tax owed | $0.00
= Refund | $64.00
```

Filing also starts a habit you will keep for life, and it is good practice while your return is simple. Your state may have its own filing rules; check your state's department of revenue.

> [!tip] Social Security and Medicare are not refunded when you file. Only federal income tax withholding comes back.

```takeaways
- For 2026, a dependent with only wages must file if earned income is more than $16,100.
- $400 or more of net self-employment earnings means you must file.
- If income tax was withheld and you owe none, file to get it back.
- Use the IRS "Do I need to file?" tool for anything unusual, like investment income.
```
