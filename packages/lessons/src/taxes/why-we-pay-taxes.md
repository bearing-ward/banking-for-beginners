Before you had a job, you were already paying taxes. Every time you bought a snack or a phone case, a few cents of **sales tax** went to your state or city. Once you start working, you will see more kinds of taxes, and they take a bigger bite. Knowing what each one is and what it pays for makes them less of a mystery.

## What taxes pay for

A **tax** is money people and businesses must pay to the government. Governments use it to pay for things we share: public schools, roads, police and fire departments, parks, the military, and programs like Social Security and Medicare that support older and disabled Americans. Your school, the roads you drive on, and the lifeguard at a public pool are paid for partly with taxes.

## The four taxes you will meet first

**Income tax** is a share of what you earn. The federal government charges it, most states do too, and some cities add their own. It is figured once a year, but your employer takes some out of each paycheck so you pay as you go.

**Payroll taxes** are Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%), also called **FICA**. They come out of every paycheck from the first dollar, and your employer pays the same amount again.

**Sales tax** is added at the register when you buy things. The rate depends on your state and city, and some states have none.

**Property tax** is paid by people who own homes and land, mostly to fund local schools and services. Renters pay it too, indirectly, because it is part of the cost of the building.

```table Taxes you will meet first
| Tax | What it is charged on | Who collects it | When you see it |
| Income tax | What you earn | Federal, most states, some cities | Paychecks and your tax return |
| Payroll tax (FICA) | Wages, from the first dollar | Federal | Every paycheck |
| Sales tax | Things you buy | Most states and many cities | The receipt |
| Property tax | Homes and land | Local governments | Homeowners' bills; built into rent |
```

## Taxes on one paycheck

Here is where the taxes went on a $300 paycheck for a teen who earns too little in the year to owe federal income tax.

```ledger Taxes on a $300 paycheck
~ Gross pay | $300.00
Social Security, 6.2% | $18.60
+ Medicare, 1.45% | $4.35
+ Federal income tax | $0.00
= Taxes paid | $22.95
```

## Taxes at the register

Sales tax rates vary a lot from place to place, so check your receipt to see yours. Here is how it works with an example rate of 7%.

```ledger New shoes, with an example sales tax of 7%
~ Sales tax rate (example) | 7%
Price on the tag | $60.00
+ Sales tax, 7% of $60 | $4.20
= What you pay | $64.20
```

## Your state matters

Where you live changes what you pay. Some states have no income tax, some have no sales tax, and rates differ everywhere. Your state's department of revenue (sometimes called the department of taxation) has a website with its rules.

> [!tip] Taxes also come with a right: filing a return is how you get back income tax that was withheld but not owed. The next lessons show how.

```takeaways
- Taxes pay for shared things: schools, roads, emergency services, Social Security, and Medicare.
- Income tax and payroll taxes come out of your paycheck; sales tax shows up at the register.
- Payroll taxes (6.2% and 1.45%) apply from your first dollar; income tax depends on how much you earn in the year.
- State and local taxes differ; look up yours.
```
