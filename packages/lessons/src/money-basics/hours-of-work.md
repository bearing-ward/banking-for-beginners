A $180 pair of sneakers sounds like one number. If you earn $15.00 an hour, it is really about 13 hours of work: most of two shifts. Thinking of prices in **hours of work** connects what you buy to the time you gave up to earn it.

## Start with what you keep, not what you earn

The hourly wage on your job offer is your gross pay. Some of it goes to taxes before it reaches you. At minimum, 6.2% goes to Social Security and 1.45% to Medicare, so you keep at most about 92% of each hour. Some people also have federal, state, or local income tax taken out, which lowers it more.

Kenji, 17, works 20 hours a week at a pizza place for $15.00 an hour. His employer withholds no income tax.

```ledger Kenji's week
~ Hours worked | 20 × $15.00
Gross pay | $300.00
- Social Security (6.2%) | $18.60
- Medicare (1.45%) | $4.35
= Net pay | $277.05
~ Net pay per hour | About $13.85
```

Kenji takes home about $13.85 for each hour he works. That is the number he divides by.

## Do the division

Hours of work is the price divided by what you keep per hour. For Kenji:

```table What things cost Kenji in hours
| Item | Price | Hours of work at $13.85 |
| New sneakers | $180.00 | 13.0 |
| A video game | $70.00 | 5.1 |
| Coffee, 5 days a week | $30.00 a week | 2.2 a week |
| A new phone | $1,200.00 | 86.6 |
```

The coffee looks small next to the phone. But 2.2 hours a week, over a year, is more than 110 hours: almost six weeks of Kenji's part-time job.

> [!note] Wages depend on where you live. In 2026, the federal minimum wage is $7.25 an hour, and many states and cities set a higher one. Look up yours on the [Department of Labor's state minimum wage page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/state). Whether you owe state income tax depends on your state too.

## Use it as a gut check, not a rule

Hours of work does not tell you what to buy. Thirteen hours for sneakers you will wear every day for a year might feel like a great deal. Five hours for a game you will finish in a weekend might not. The point is to ask the question before you tap "buy."

Try the calculator beside this lesson with your own wage and something you have been eyeing.

```takeaways
- Hours of work = price ÷ what you take home per hour.
- Use your take-home pay per hour, not your hourly wage. Taxes take at least 7.65%.
- Small repeat purchases add up to many hours over a year.
- Ask "Is this worth that many hours of my time?" and then decide.
```

```quiz
? You take home $12 an hour. How many hours of work is a $60 jacket?
```
