---
title: Opportunity cost
level: starter
summary: Every time you spend money, you give up something else you could have done with it. Seeing that trade-off makes choices easier.
---

You have $120 from babysitting. You can buy a hoodie, put it toward a used bike, or keep it for your car fund. Whatever you pick, you give up the others. The best thing you give up is called the **opportunity cost** of your choice.

Opportunity cost is not a fee anyone charges you. It is a way of seeing the real price of something: not only the dollars, but what those dollars could have been instead.

## Name the trade-off out loud

Jaylen, 16, makes $15.00 an hour bagging groceries. He is saving for a $600 used laptop and has $360 so far. A friend asks him to go to a theme park this Saturday.

```ledger Jaylen's Saturday
Theme park ticket | $85.00
+ Food and drinks in the park | $30.00
+ His share of gas | $10.00
= Cost of the day | $125.00
```

The day costs $125. That is not only $125. It also pushes his laptop back.

```ledger The laptop fund, with and without the trip
Saved so far | $360.00
- The theme park day | $125.00
= Left in the laptop fund | $235.00
~ Still needed for a $600 laptop | $365.00
~ At $60 saved a week | About 6 more weeks
```

Without the trip, Jaylen needs $240 more, which is 4 weeks at $60 a week. With it, he needs $365, which is about 6 more weeks. So the real question is: "Is a day with my friends worth waiting about two extra weeks for the laptop?"

There is no wrong answer. Maybe the day with his friends matters more. The point is that he decides with both sides in view.

## Time has an opportunity cost too

Opportunity cost is not only about money. An extra shift on Saturday earns money but costs you the game, the practice, or the rest. A summer job pays now; a summer class or unpaid internship might pay more later. Weigh what you give up on both sides.

```table Two ways to spend a Saturday
| | Pick up a shift | Go to the game |
| You get | $120 in pay for 8 hours at $15.00, before taxes | Time with friends |
| You give up | The game | $120 in pay, before taxes |
```

> [!tip] Before a bigger purchase, finish the sentence "If I buy this, I will not be able to ___." If the blank does not bother you, go ahead.

```takeaways
- Opportunity cost is the best thing you give up when you choose something else.
- The real price of a purchase includes the goal it pushes back.
- Time has opportunity costs too: shifts, classes, and rest all trade against each other.
- Name the trade-off out loud, then decide. Either choice can be the right one.
```

```quiz
? Ana has $50. She buys a game instead of going to a concert or saving it. Her next favorite option was the concert. What is her opportunity cost?
- The $50 game
+ The concert
- Nothing, because she wanted the game
> Opportunity cost is the best option you gave up. For Ana, that was the concert.

? Why does it help to think about opportunity cost?
- It tells you never to spend money
- It lowers the price of things
+ It shows what a purchase really trades away, so you can choose on purpose
> Opportunity cost does not say "do not buy." It makes the trade-off visible so the choice is yours.
```
