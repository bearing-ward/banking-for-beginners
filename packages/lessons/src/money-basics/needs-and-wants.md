---
title: Needs and wants
level: starter
summary: How to sort what you spend into needs and wants, and why the line between them is not always where you think.
---

Every dollar you spend goes to one of two places. A **need** is something you have to pay for to stay safe, healthy, and able to work or go to school: food, a place to live, basic clothes, getting to your job. A **want** is something that makes life better or more fun but that you could go without: concert tickets, a new game, eating out.

Wants are not bad. They are part of why you work. The point of sorting is to make sure the needs get paid first, so the wants never come out of money you needed for something else.

## The same thing can be both

Many purchases are a need with a want on top. You need shoes; you want the $140 pair. You need a way to get to your job; you want to drive instead of taking the bus. Splitting the price shows you how much of it is the want.

Leo, 17, works at a grocery store and needs black non-slip shoes for his shifts. He likes a $95 pair, but a basic pair that meets the dress code costs $45.

```ledger Leo's work shoes
~ What the job requires | Black, non-slip
Basic pair that meets the dress code (need) | $45.00
+ Upgrade to the pair he likes (want) | $50.00
= Price of the pair he likes | $95.00
```

Leo can still buy the $95 pair. Now he knows $50 of it is a want, and he can decide if it is worth $50 to him.

## Needs change as you grow up

Right now, a parent or guardian may cover most of your needs. That will shift. When you move out, rent, groceries, and a phone bill become your needs, and they take the first bite of every paycheck. Practicing now, while the stakes are small, makes that move easier.

```table Sorting a month of Leo's spending
| Item | Need or want? | Why |
| Bus pass to work | Need | He cannot earn without getting there |
| Lunch on shift days | Need, partly | Food is a need; buying it instead of packing it is a want |
| Streaming service | Want | Nice to have, easy to pause |
| Phone bill share | Need | His job texts him his schedule |
| New headphones | Want | His old pair still works |
```

> [!tip] When you are not sure, ask: "What happens if I do not buy this for a month?" If the answer is "nothing much," it is a want.

## Try it this week

Look at the last ten things you bought, in your bank app or your receipts. Mark each one need, want, or both. You are not grading yourself. You are learning where your money really goes, which is the first step to a budget.

```takeaways
- A need keeps you safe, healthy, and able to work or learn. A want makes life better but can wait.
- Many purchases are a need plus a want; split the price to see how much is the want.
- Pay for needs first, then choose wants on purpose.
```

```quiz
? Maya needs a laptop for school. A basic one costs $350 and the one she likes costs $900. How much of the $900 is a want?
- $900
- $350
+ $550
> The need is a laptop that does the job, $350. The extra $550 is the want. She may still choose it, but now she knows its real price.

? Which of these is most clearly a need for a teen with a job?
+ A way to get to work
- A streaming subscription
- A new phone case
> Getting to work is what lets you earn. The others are nice to have and can wait.
```
