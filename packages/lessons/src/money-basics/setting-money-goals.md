title: Setting money goals
level: starter
summary: Turn "I want to save more" into a goal with a number, a date, and a weekly plan you can actually follow.
---

"I should save money" is a wish. "I will save $900 for a used e-bike by June 1" is a **goal**. The difference is a number and a date. With those two, you can work out exactly what to do each week, and you can tell if you are on track.

## Make it specific

A good money goal answers four questions:

- **What** is it for? A car, a trip, a laptop, a cushion for surprises.
- **How much** does it cost? Look up a real price, plus tax or fees.
- **By when** do you need it?
- **How much per week** gets you there? Divide the amount by the weeks.

Priya, 16, works weekends at a frozen yogurt shop. She wants a used e-bike so she can get to her job without a ride. She found one listed for $850, and she wants $50 extra for a lock and a helmet.

```ledger Priya's e-bike goal
Used e-bike | $850.00
+ Lock and helmet | $50.00
= Goal | $900.00
- Already saved | $180.00
= Still to save | $720.00
~ Weeks until June 1 | 18
~ Per week | $40.00
```

$720 over 18 weeks is $40 a week. Priya takes home about $110 a week, so $40 is doable without giving up everything else.

## Short, medium, and long goals

It helps to have goals at different distances. Short goals keep you motivated. Long goals are where steady saving pays off most.

```table Goals at different distances
| | Example | Time | Where to keep the money |
| Short | Concert tickets, $120 | A few weeks | Checking or savings |
| Medium | A car down payment, $2,000 | 1 to 3 years | Savings |
| Long | Retirement | Decades | Retirement account, invested |
```

Money you need soon belongs somewhere safe, like a savings account at an FDIC-insured bank. Money you will not touch for many years can be invested, where values go up and down along the way.

## When the plan slips

Some weeks you will save less. A shift gets cut, or a friend has a birthday. That is normal. Recalculate: divide what is left by the weeks that are left. If the new weekly number is too high, move the date or lower the goal. A goal you adjust is still a goal.

> [!tip] Give the money its own home. Many banks let you open a second savings account or a named "bucket" for free. Name it "E-bike" and watch it grow.

Write down one goal today with a number and a date. Then work out the weekly amount.

```takeaways
- A goal has a number and a date; a wish does not.
- Divide what is left by the weeks you have to get a weekly target.
- Keep short-term goal money somewhere safe, not invested.
- When life happens, recalculate instead of quitting.
```
