Most people try to save whatever is left at the end of the month. Usually nothing is left. **Paying yourself first** flips the order: the day your paycheck lands, you move a set amount to savings, and you spend from what remains.

It works because you never see the saved money as spending money. It is gone from your checking account before you can miss it.

## Pick an amount and make it automatic

Start with a share of each paycheck that you will not feel too much, like 10% to 20%. Then set up an **automatic transfer** in your bank app from checking to savings on payday. Some employers can also split your **direct deposit** so part of each check goes straight into savings.

Destiny, 17, works at a movie theater and is paid every two weeks. She has her employer send $40 of each check to savings.

```ledger Destiny's paycheck, split on payday
~ Pay schedule | Every two weeks
Net pay | $268.40
- Sent straight to savings | $40.00
= Left in checking to spend | $228.40
```

Destiny plans her spending around $228.40. The $40 is not part of the plan.

## Small amounts grow

$40 every two weeks does not feel like much. Over a year it adds up, and in a savings account that pays interest, the money earns a little more on its own.

```ledger Destiny's savings after one year
~ Paychecks in a year | 26
~ Saved per paycheck | $40.00
Total she put in | $1,040.00
+ Interest earned, roughly, at about 4% a year | $20.00
= In savings after a year | $1,060.00
```

That interest figure is rough: savings rates change, and many accounts pay less. The real win is the $1,040 she would likely have spent otherwise. Try the calculator beside this lesson to see what steady saving looks like over 5 or 10 years.

## What if money is tight?

Start smaller. $5 a paycheck builds the habit, and you can raise it when you get a raise or more hours. If you have a bill that is due, pay it on time first; late fees cost more than you would save.

> [!tip] When your pay goes up, raise your savings transfer the same day, before you get used to spending the extra.

```takeaways
- Pay yourself first: move savings out on payday, then spend what is left.
- Automate it with a bank transfer or a split direct deposit.
- Small, steady amounts add up; start with whatever you can.
- Raise the amount when your pay goes up.
```
