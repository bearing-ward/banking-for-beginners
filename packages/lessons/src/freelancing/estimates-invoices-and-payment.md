A customer saying "send me the bill" is not the same as money arriving. **An estimate** describes the expected price before work starts. **An invoice** requests payment for the agreed work. These fictional examples show how the documents connect; they are not a legal contract template.

## Agree before starting

Maya, 18, will design a poster for a neighborhood event. She and the organizer agree in writing on the design, one revision, delivery date, a $180 total price, and payment dates. In this example, there are no additional taxes or fees. Real businesses must check applicable tax rules.

They agree on $60 before work begins and $120 after the final design is accepted, due by the date on the invoice. They also agree how cancellation and any refund would work. A **deposit** is an upfront payment; it is not automatically nonrefundable. Ask about the terms before paying or accepting one.

```table Maya's estimate becomes an agreement
| Item | Agreed detail |
| Deliverable | One event poster in the specified digital formats |
| Included changes | One revision round |
| Total | $180 |
| Upfront payment | $60 before starting |
| Final payment | $120 by the agreed invoice date |
| Additional work | New written approval of price and timing before starting |
```

If you are under your state's age of legal adulthood, ask a trusted adult and qualified local adviser about contracts and payment accounts. Do not misstate your age to open an account or use someone else's identity. Business and platform requirements vary.

## Make the invoice easy to check

Give each invoice a unique number. Include your business contact details, the customer, date, description of work, agreed total, credits for prior payments, remaining amount, due date, and agreed payment method. Keep sensitive identity and banking information out of public documents. The [SBA's finance guidance](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/manage-your-finances) explains why accurate records and payment management matter.

```ledger Maya's final invoice, number 014
Agreed poster price | $180.00
- Deposit already received | $60.00
= Remaining payment due | $120.00
```

The deposit is part of the $180 price, not extra income on top of it. Mark the invoice paid when the payment is confirmed. Keep records of processing fees, refunds, and any chargebacks separately. A screenshot claiming payment is not proof that money reached your account.

## Follow up without changing the deal

If payment is late, first check your own records and the agreed due date. Send a calm reminder with a copy of the invoice and ask whether there is a billing problem. Do not invent a late fee after the work is done. Any fee must comply with applicable law and agreed terms.

```message Maya's payment reminder
Channel: email
From: Maya
Subject: Follow-up on invoice 014
Hello, invoice 014 for the event poster shows $120 still due as of the agreed payment date. The invoice and payment instructions are attached. Could you confirm when payment will be sent, or let me know if you see an error?
```

If the client disputes the work or still does not pay, keep the agreement, delivery records, and messages. Review your terms before pausing further work. Ask a local small-business adviser or legal aid service about appropriate next steps rather than making threats. A deposit reduces exposure but cannot guarantee payment.

Keep payment records even if no tax form arrives. The [IRS recordkeeping guide](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep) describes documents that support income and expenses.

```takeaways
- Agree on deliverables, total price, payment dates, and cancellation terms before starting.
- Credit deposits against the invoice total so you do not bill or count them twice.
- Confirm payments in your account and keep invoices, receipts, and delivery records.
- Follow up on overdue invoices using the agreed terms; do not invent fees later.
```
