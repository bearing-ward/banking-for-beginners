title: Challenge your freelance project plan
level: deep-dive
summary: Quote a small project, respond to extra work, and compare the final payment with the time and costs it took.
---

Can a project that pays $240 still be a poor deal for your time? This fictional design job combines pricing, written scope, invoicing, and keeping records. The amounts are before taxes and exclude sales tax for the exercise; actual tax rules must be checked.

## Try the quote first

A neighborhood club asks Ari, 18, for one flyer and one revision round. Ari expects six hours for design, one hour for messages and billing, and one hour for revisions. Supplies and licensed assets will cost $24. The agreed project price would be $240.

Before reading on, work out the total hours and what remains per hour after those costs. Then decide how Ari should respond if the client asks for two extra social-media graphics.

```ledger Ari's expected project return
Agreed project price | $240.00
- Supplies and licensed assets | $24.00
= Return before taxes and other overhead | $216.00
~ Total expected hours | 6 design + 1 admin + 1 revisions = 8
~ Return per expected hour | $216 ÷ 8 = $27
```

The $27 is not a guaranteed take-home wage. It still has to support taxes, any other business overhead, and unpaid time between projects. Ari needs a clear agreement describing what the $240 includes.

## Handle the change before doing it

The client asks for two additional graphics. Ari estimates two extra hours and $6 in additional licensed assets. Ari offers a $66 addition. The client can accept, decline, or discuss a different scope before work starts.

```message Ari's response to a change request
Channel: email
From: Ari
The original $240 quote covers one flyer and one revision round. I can add the two social graphics for $66, making the total $306. It would move delivery to Friday. Please confirm the new price, scope, and date in writing before I start the additional work.
```

A **change request** alters the agreed work. Do not assume the client accepted a new price because you sent a message. In this story, the client confirms it. They also confirm that one revision round applies to the added graphics.

## Check the actual result

The client pays a $100 deposit and later pays the $206 invoice balance. Ari's total costs are $30. The whole job takes ten hours as estimated. There are no payment fees or other expenses in this exercise.

```ledger Ari's final project result
Deposit received | $100.00
+ Final payment received | $206.00
= Total customer payments | $306.00
- Supplies and licensed assets | $30.00
= Return before taxes and other overhead | $276.00
~ Actual total hours | 10
~ Return per actual hour | $27.60
```

If the job had taken twelve hours instead, the same $276 would be $23 per hour. Keep actual time records, including messages and revisions, to improve your next estimate. Record the deposit as part of the price rather than adding it again.

The [SBA finance guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/manage-your-finances) covers tracking business finances; the [IRS recordkeeping guide](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep) explains supporting records. Before doing paid work as a minor, get help checking contracts, account requirements, and applicable local rules.

```takeaways
- Include admin and revisions when estimating your hours.
- Agree on additional work, price, and timing before starting it.
- Compare the actual time and costs with the quote after finishing.
- The amount left before taxes and overhead is not guaranteed take-home pay.
```
