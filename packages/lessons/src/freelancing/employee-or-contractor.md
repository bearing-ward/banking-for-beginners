title: Employee or contractor?
level: next-step
summary: A job title or tax form does not decide your status. Check the actual work arrangement and know where to ask for help.

A shop offers 17-year-old Mina a job editing product photos. The owner calls her a freelancer and says she will receive a 1099. Mina wonders whether that settles who handles taxes and which work protections apply. It does not.

**Classification** means deciding whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor under a particular law. An **independent contractor** runs an independent business. An **employee** works in an employment relationship. The actual facts matter more than the title in a message or agreement.

## Look beyond the label

For federal employment taxes, the IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship between the parties. Those include who controls how work is done, business expenses and opportunities, and whether the relationship is ongoing. No single fact, such as choosing your hours or using your own laptop, settles every case. Read the [IRS classification guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee).

```table Facts Mina should record
| Question | Why write down the answer? |
| Who decides how the editing must be done? | Instructions and supervision are part of the relationship |
| Who sets prices and pays business expenses? | The financial arrangement matters |
| Can Mina take other clients and negotiate projects? | This helps describe whether she operates her own business |
| Is this continuing work or a defined project? | The intended and actual relationship matters |
| What does the agreement say, and what happens in practice? | A label may conflict with the actual work |
```

Getting a **Form 1099-NEC**, a report of certain payments, does not prove you are a contractor. Getting paid through an app, working remotely, or agreeing to a contract does not decide it either. Keep the offer, agreement, instructions, hours, and payment records.

## Different laws can use different tests

The IRS tax test is not the same as the federal Fair Labor Standards Act test used for minimum wage and overtime. Under that labor law, the central question is whether the worker is economically dependent on the business for work or is in business for themselves. State laws can use different tests for wages, unemployment insurance, or other protections.

A worker's status under one law does not settle every other law. A true independent contractor is not covered by the FLSA's employee minimum-wage and overtime provisions, but that does not mean contractors have no legal protections. The [Department of Labor's classification page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification) explains federal wage-law issues. Check your [state labor office](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/contacts) for state questions.

```table Same label, questions still open
| Offer | What you can conclude | What you cannot conclude |
| The shop says Mina is a contractor | That is the shop's stated treatment | That the treatment is correct under every law |
| Mina chooses some work hours | There is some scheduling flexibility | That flexibility alone makes her a contractor |
| The shop sends a 1099 | It reported payments that way | That Mina gave up employee protections |
```

Classification rules and enforcement guidance can change. Use the current agency guidance when a real question comes up, rather than treating this page as a legal checklist that decides your case.

## Ask before guessing

Mina can ask the shop how it determined the classification. If the answer does not fit the work, she can get help. For a federal tax determination, either a worker or business can ask the IRS using **Form SS-8**. For federal wage questions, contact the Wage and Hour Division. State agencies and legal aid can help identify the right process for other issues.

```message Mina asks about the arrangement
Channel: email
From: Mina
Subject: Questions before I start photo editing
You described this as contractor work. Could you explain the work arrangement and how you determined that status? I would like to understand who controls the editing process, which expenses I cover, and how pay will be reported. I will keep a copy of our agreement and work instructions.
```

Because Mina is under 18, she should also ask a trusted adult to help check age restrictions and the agreement. A contractor label does not give someone permission to ignore applicable teen work rules. Do not assume you can fix a classification problem by picking a different tax form yourself. Ask the relevant agency or a qualified tax or legal adviser about the facts and filing deadlines.

```takeaways
- A 1099, app, flexible schedule, or contract label does not decide your worker status.
- Federal tax, federal labor, and state laws can use different classification tests.
- Keep records of how the work actually happens, including instructions, hours, expenses, and pay.
- Ask the IRS about tax classification and labor agencies about wage and state protections.
```
