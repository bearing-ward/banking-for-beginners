---
title: Pricing your work
level: next-step
summary: Count the whole job, including messages, revisions, and expenses, before you agree to a price.
---

Jules, 17, is offered $90 to edit a short video for a neighborhood event. He expects three hours of editing, so the offer sounds like $30 an hour. But editing is only part of the job.

Your **fee** is what the client pays. Your **effective hourly earnings** are what remains after business costs, divided by all the time the job takes. In this lesson, that amount is before taxes. It is not the same as an employee's hourly wage or take-home pay.

## Count all the work

Include planning, client messages, file organization, revisions, exporting, delivery, and collecting payment. A **revision** is a change to work you already made. Put a limit on included revisions and agree on what extra work will cost before doing it.

```table Jules estimates the whole job
| Task | Expected time |
| Discuss the brief and organize files | 1 hour |
| Edit the video | 3 hours |
| One round of agreed revisions | 1 hour |
| Export, deliver, and send the invoice | 1 hour |
| Total | 6 hours |
```

The offer is $90 divided by six hours, or $15 an hour before costs and taxes. If the client expects unlimited changes, Jules cannot make a useful time estimate yet. He needs to clarify the work first.

## Subtract the costs

Jules needs a $12 licensed music track and expects $6 in payment-processing fees. These fictional costs belong to this job. Costs can also include supplies, travel, software, equipment wear, and part of ongoing business expenses. Check the actual costs rather than assuming a payment app is free.

```ledger What the $90 offer leaves
Client fee | $90.00
- Licensed music track | $12.00
- Payment-processing fees | $6.00
= Left before taxes | $72.00
# Six total hours means $72 ÷ 6 = $12 per hour before taxes.
```

That $72 must cover Jules's time and leave room for taxes and other business needs. He should not describe it as $90 of profit. The [Small Business Administration's planning guide](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) explains estimating costs and checking the market.

## Quote a defined job

For practice, Jules chooses a target of $18 per total working hour before tax, after the listed job costs. This is a hypothetical planning target, not a recommended market rate. Six hours times $18 is $108. Adding the estimated $18 of costs gives a $126 quote. If processing fees depend on the price, he must recalculate them using the provider's actual terms.

```ledger Jules builds a revised quote
~ Estimated total working time | 6 hours
Time allowance, 6 × $18 | $108.00
+ Estimated job costs | $18.00
= Proposed fee | $126.00
# This example assumes the costs stay at $18 for the revised quote.
```

Jules offers a $126 fee for the defined video, one revision round, and the agreed delivery date. The client may accept, decline, or ask for a smaller job. If the budget is $90, Jules could discuss a shorter edit or fewer deliverables instead of quietly promising the same six-hour job.

A fixed fee gives the client a clear price but leaves you with the time risk. An hourly price needs agreement about which hours count, how you track them, and whether there is a spending cap. Neither approach guarantees profit or payment. Compare similar work, your costs, and what the client actually needs before agreeing.

After the job, record the actual time and costs. If it took eight hours and still left $72, the effective rate was $9 per hour before taxes. Use that evidence to improve the next estimate. For help planning a small business, find an [SBA resource partner](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/local-assistance/).

Set aside money for taxes as you get paid. Estimated payments may be due during the year, not only when you file. The separate gig-work and tax lessons explain that next step.

```takeaways
- Count admin, delivery, and revisions along with the visible work.
- Subtract costs, then divide by total hours to compare effective earnings before taxes.
- Define the job and included revisions before quoting a fixed price.
- Compare estimates with actual time and costs so the next quote is better informed.
```
