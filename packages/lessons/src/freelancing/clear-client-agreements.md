title: Clear client agreements
level: next-step
summary: Agree on the work, price, changes, and payment before starting a client project. Ask for help with terms you do not understand.

Sam, 17, agrees to make a flyer for a local car wash. The client says, "Make something good and we will sort out pay later." Sam is ready to draw, but neither person knows what the promise includes yet.

A **client agreement** records what each person promises. Clear written terms help prevent disagreements about work and payment. They are useful even when the client is someone you know. This lesson is a planning checklist, not a legal contract template.

## Describe what finished means

**Scope** is the work included in the price. Name the deliverables, file formats, quantity, and what the client must supply. A deadline should include time for the client's feedback, not only your own work.

```table Questions Sam settles before drawing
| Topic | Example details to agree |
| People | Who is ordering, approving, and paying for the work? |
| Scope | One digital flyer, one size, delivered as PDF and PNG |
| Client materials | Client supplies approved text and a logo by November 20 |
| Timing | First draft November 24, feedback November 26, final November 28 |
| Revisions | One round of edits to that flyer; a new design needs a new quote |
| Approval | Client checks names, dates, prices, and spelling before final delivery |
```

Confirm what happens if materials or feedback arrive late. Put new deadlines in writing. Do not promise a deadline you cannot meet around school and other work.

## Agree about money and changes

Record the total fee, any deposit, when the balance is due, how payment works, and who covers payment fees or agreed expenses. An **invoice** is a request for payment that identifies the work, amount, and due date. Keep the agreement, invoice, and payment records.

```ledger Sam's agreed $80 fee
~ Total project fee | $80.00
Deposit already paid | $20.00
+ Balance due at agreed delivery | $60.00
= Total paid if both payments arrive | $80.00
# A sent invoice is not the same as money received.
```

Agree before work starts on what happens if either person cancels or pauses the project. Cover payment for work already completed, expenses already approved, refunds of unused deposits, and what work may be used afterward. Do not assume a deposit is automatically yours to keep. Cancellation terms and enforceability depend on the agreement and applicable law.

If the client adds work, explain the new fee and timing and get agreement before doing it. A written change protects both people from a surprise bill or a surprise workload.

```message Sam responds to an extra request
Channel: email
From: Sam
Subject: Additional flyer sizes
Our agreed fee covers one flyer size and one revision round. Two extra sizes would add $30 and move delivery to November 30. Please confirm whether you want that change before I begin the extra versions. If not, I will continue with the original scope and deadline.
```

## Discuss ownership and permitted use

For creative work, discuss who will own the work or have permission to use it, which uses are allowed, whether editable source files are included, and whether you may show it in a portfolio. Do not assume a payment automatically answers every ownership question. Check permission for photos, fonts, music, and logos before using them. Avoid promising rights to material you do not control.

Ask someone qualified to explain terms that transfer broad rights, require you to pay the client's legal costs, or make you responsible for losses beyond the project. A short job can still come with serious obligations. The [SBA's local assistance directory](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/local-assistance/) can help you find business guidance; legal questions may need a lawyer.

## Under 18? Get help before agreeing

**Capacity** means legal ability to enter a binding agreement. Rules for minors and exceptions depend on state law and the type of contract. Do not assume being under 18 makes every promise unenforceable, or that an adult's signature automatically fixes every issue. The [Washington courts' lesson on minors and contracts](https://www.courts.wa.gov/education/lessons/?fa=education_lessons.dspPlan&plan=MinorsAndContracts) illustrates why age and exceptions matter; its rules are specific to Washington.

Ask a parent, guardian, or another trusted adult to review the plan with you. For your own state's rules or a significant agreement, seek local legal help. An adult who signs may take on obligations, so everyone should understand their role. Check any platform's age requirements and do not misstate your age.

Before starting, make sure both people have the same written terms and a copy. If you cannot explain the scope, payment dates, cancellation terms, and rights in your own words, pause and ask questions.

```takeaways
- Define deliverables, deadlines, client responsibilities, and included revisions before starting.
- Agree on the fee, payment dates, expenses, cancellation, and changes in writing.
- Discuss ownership and allowed uses, including source files and portfolio use.
- Minors' contract rules vary. Get adult and qualified legal help when needed before agreeing.
```

```quiz
> Both people need to know what the price buys and when payment is due. Clear terms reduce avoidable disputes.

+ Agree in writing on any added price and deadline before doing the extra work
- Assume the original fee covers every new request
- Finish everything and announce a new price afterward
> Changes can affect time and cost. Give the client a clear choice before expanding the project.

? You are 17 and an agreement has unfamiliar ownership and cancellation terms. What should you do?
- Assume your age means none of the terms matter
- Sign because an online template must be right for every state
+ Get trusted adult help and appropriate local legal guidance before agreeing
> Contract capacity and enforceability vary by state and circumstance. Understand the obligations instead of relying on age or a template as a shortcut.
```
