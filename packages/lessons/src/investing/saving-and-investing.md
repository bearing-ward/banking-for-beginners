---
title: Saving and investing do different jobs
level: starter
summary: Money you need soon belongs in savings, where it is safe. Money you will not touch for years can be invested, where it can grow but also fall.
tool: emergency-fund
---

Sofia, 17, works weekends at a coffee shop. She has $1,800 saved and two goals: a $1,000 used laptop for senior year, and "something for later." She asks whether she should put all of it in the stock market. The answer depends on when she needs each dollar.

**Saving** means keeping money somewhere safe and easy to reach, like a savings account at a bank or credit union. It earns a little interest, and the balance does not go down. **Investing** means buying something, such as shares of a company, that you expect to grow in value over many years. It can earn much more than a savings account, but its value goes up and down along the way, and you can lose money.

```table Two jobs for your money
| | Saving | Investing |
| Best for | Money you need within a few years | Money you will not need for 5+ years |
| Can the value drop? | No, if the bank is FDIC insured | Yes, sometimes by a lot |
| Typical growth | Low, steady interest | Higher on average, but uneven |
| Examples | Savings account, CD | Stocks, bonds, funds |
```

## Match the money to the timeline

The question to ask about any dollar is "When will I need this?" If the answer is "soon," a drop in value could hit right when you need to spend it. If the answer is "in 20 years," a bad year has time to recover.

In 2026, the FDIC insures deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership type. Investments are not FDIC insured, even when you buy them through a bank.

Sofia splits her money by timeline:

```ledger Sofia's $1,800, sorted by when she needs it
Laptop in 4 months (save) | $1,000.00
+ Emergency cushion (save) | $500.00
+ Money for 10+ years from now (invest) | $300.00
= Total | $1,800.00
```

## Savings first, then investing

Most people build a small **emergency fund** before they invest: cash for a surprise like a phone repair or a missed shift. Without it, an emergency could force you to sell investments on a bad day, at a loss. Once the cushion is in place, the money you will not need for years can start working harder.

> [!note] Investing is not only for adults with big paychecks. It is a way to use time, and teens have more of it than anyone. Later lessons show how.

```takeaways
- Saving keeps money safe for near-term goals; investing aims for growth over many years.
- Ask "When will I need this money?" before deciding where it goes.
- Bank deposits can be FDIC insured; investments are not, and their value can fall.
- Build an emergency cushion before you invest.
```
