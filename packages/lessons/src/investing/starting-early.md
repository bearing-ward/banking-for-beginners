---
title: Why starting young matters
level: next-step
summary: Compound growth rewards time more than almost anything else. Ten years of head start can matter more than decades of extra deposits.
tool: compound-growth
---

When an investment grows, the growth itself starts to grow. That is **compound growth**. In the first few years it looks slow. Later, the growth on past growth becomes bigger than what you put in. The longer your money has, the more of those later years it gets, and that is the one advantage a teenager has over every adult.

## Same deposit, different start

Ava and Ben each invest $100 a month in a low-cost stock index fund until age 66. Ava starts at 16. Ben starts at 26. To keep the math clear, assume the fund earns 6% a year, compounded monthly.

> [!warning] 6% is an assumption for this example, not a promise. Real investments go up and down, some years by a lot, and nobody knows what future returns will be.

```ledger Ava: $100 a month from 16 to 66
~ Assumed return | 6% a year
~ Years | 50
You put in | $60,000.00
+ Growth | $318,719.11
= Balance at 66 | $378,719.11
```

```ledger Ben: $100 a month from 26 to 66
~ Assumed return | 6% a year
~ Years | 40
You put in | $48,000.00
+ Growth | $151,149.07
= Balance at 66 | $199,149.07
```

Ava put in $12,000 more than Ben, but she ended with $179,570.04 more. Those 10 extra years came at the start, so their growth had the longest time to compound.

## Ten years that keep working

Now meet Camila. She invests $100 a month from 16 to 26, then stops for good and never adds another dollar. The money stays invested and keeps growing at the same assumed 6%.

```ledger Camila: $100 a month from 16 to 26 only
You put in, ages 16 to 26 | $12,000.00
+ Growth by age 26 | $4,387.93
= Balance at 26 | $16,387.93
+ Growth from 26 to 66, no new deposits | $163,182.05
= Balance at 66 | $179,569.98
```

```table Three investors at an assumed 6% a year
| | Years investing | Total put in | Balance at 66 |
| Ava | 16 to 66 | $60,000 | $378,719.11 |
| Ben | 26 to 66 | $48,000 | $199,149.07 |
| Camila | 16 to 26 | $12,000 | $179,569.98 |
```

Camila put in a quarter of what Ben did and ended up close to him. With a different assumed return, the gap changes, but the pattern holds: early dollars have the most time to grow.

## What you can do now

You do not need $100 a month to start. Try the calculator with your own numbers: $25 a month, a lower return, a shorter time. Keep the return modest, and remember that a real balance will bounce around instead of rising in a smooth line. The [compound interest calculator](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator) on Investor.gov is another way to explore.

```takeaways
- Compound growth means growth earns growth, so time is the biggest ingredient.
- Starting at 16 instead of 26 can roughly double the result, even with the same monthly amount.
- The returns in these examples are assumptions; real investments rise and fall.
- Starting small now beats waiting to start big later.
```
