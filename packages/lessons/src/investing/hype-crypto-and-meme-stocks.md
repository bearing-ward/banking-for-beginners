Your feed is full of people who say they turned $200 into $20,000 with a coin or a hot stock. Some of those stories are true. You rarely hear from the many more people who bought near the top and lost most of their money.

**Investing** usually means owning something that produces value over time, like a slice of many companies' profits, and holding it for years. **Speculating** means buying something mainly because you hope its price will jump soon, often because other people are excited about it. Speculating is not illegal, and some people choose to do it with a small amount. It is closer to a bet than to a plan.

## Volatility cuts both ways

**Volatility** is how fast and how far a price swings. Prices driven by hype, like many **cryptocurrencies** (digital currencies such as bitcoin and countless smaller coins) and **meme stocks** (stocks that spike because of social media buzz), can rise several times over in weeks and fall as fast or faster.

Kai, 17, puts $500 of his DoorDash earnings into a new coin his friends are talking about. It climbs 300%. He holds, hoping for more. Then it falls 80% from its peak.

```ledger Kai's $500 coin
Bought | $500.00
+ Rise of 300% | $1,500.00
= Value at the peak | $2,000.00
- Fall of 80% from the peak | $1,600.00
= Value after the fall | $400.00
```

At the peak, Kai felt rich. On paper, he made $1,500. In the end, he has $100 less than he started with. Swings like this are common for speculative assets, and some coins and stocks fall to nearly zero and never come back.

## FOMO and the pitch

**FOMO**, the fear of missing out, is what hype runs on. Scammers know it. In a **pump and dump**, a group talks up a cheap coin or stock, buys in first, and sells to the newcomers once the price rises, leaving them with the loss.

```message A DM after you follow a crypto account
Channel: dm
From: @CryptoKingMarcus
Bro my private group 10x'd last week. New coin launches in 2 hours, get in before it moons. Only 20 spots left. Send $250 to my wallet and I'll set you up. Guaranteed gains.
! "Guaranteed" returns: no real investment can promise this
! A countdown and limited spots to rush you
! Asks you to send money to a stranger's wallet
! A stranger reaching out first
```

## Facts to know before you buy

- Crypto held in an app or exchange is not FDIC insured. If the company fails or is hacked, you may not get it back.
- Crypto transfers usually cannot be reversed. Money sent to a scammer is almost always gone.
- Big wins you see online are a filtered sample. You see the winners, not the losers.

If you decide to speculate, a common approach is to use only a small amount you could lose entirely without it hurting your plans, and to keep your long-term money in diversified, low-cost investments. [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov) and [FINRA](https://www.finra.org) post alerts on crypto and social media investment scams, and you can report fraud to the [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/tcr).

```takeaways
- Investing means owning something that builds value over years; speculating is betting on a quick price jump.
- Hype-driven assets can rise fast and fall faster, sometimes to almost nothing.
- "Guaranteed," "act now," and "send to my wallet" are scam signals.
- Crypto is not FDIC insured, and transfers usually cannot be undone.
```
