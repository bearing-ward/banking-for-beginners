When you invest, you are buying one of a few basic things. Most of what you will see in an investment app or a retirement plan is a stock, a bond, or a fund that holds many of them.

## Stocks: owning a piece of a company

A **stock** (also called a **share**) is a small slice of ownership in a company. If the company grows and earns more, people will pay more for its shares, and the price rises. If it struggles, the price falls. Some companies also pay owners a **dividend**, a small cash payment out of their profits.

Ella, 16, uses birthday money to buy 3 shares of a sneaker company at $42.00 each. A year later the price is $46.00.

```ledger Ella's 3 shares
~ Shares | 3
Paid: 3 × $42.00 | $126.00
+ Price rise: 3 × $4.00 | $12.00
= Value a year later | $138.00
# The price could as easily have fallen. One company is a concentrated bet.
```

## Bonds: lending your money

A **bond** is a loan you make to a government or a company. They promise to pay you interest on a schedule and to pay back the amount you lent on a set date. Bonds usually swing less than stocks, but they can lose value too, for example if the borrower cannot pay or if interest rates rise.

```ledger A $1,000 city bond held for 5 years
~ Interest rate | 4% a year
~ Interest each year | $40
Amount lent and paid back at the end | $1,000.00
+ Interest: 5 years × $40 | $200.00
= Total received over 5 years | $1,200.00
```

U.S. Treasury bonds are backed by the federal government. [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov) explains the different kinds of bonds and their risks.

## Funds: a basket of many investments

A **fund** pools money from many people and buys a big basket of stocks, bonds, or both. One share of a fund can give you a small piece of hundreds of companies at once. That is diversification built in.

You will see two common kinds. A **mutual fund** is bought and sold once a day at the day's closing price. An **exchange-traded fund (ETF)** trades during the day like a stock. Both charge a yearly fee, which the next lesson covers.

```table The three building blocks
| | What you own | How you can earn | Main risk |
| Stock | Part of one company | Price rises, dividends | The company does poorly |
| Bond | A loan to a borrower | Interest | Borrower defaults, rates rise |
| Fund | A basket of many | Whatever the basket earns | The whole market falls |
```

> [!note] Many brokerages let you buy a **fractional share**, a piece of one share, for a few dollars. You do not need hundreds of dollars to start.

```takeaways
- A stock is part ownership of a company; its price rises and falls with the company and the market.
- A bond is a loan that pays interest; it usually swings less than stocks.
- A fund holds many stocks or bonds at once, which spreads out risk.
- ETFs and mutual funds are two ways to buy a fund.
```
