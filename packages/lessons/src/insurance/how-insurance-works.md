---
title: How insurance works
level: starter
summary: Insurance turns a big, unpredictable cost into a small, predictable one. Learn the four words on every policy: premium, deductible, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket maximum.
---

Most bad luck is rare but expensive: a car crash, a hospital stay, a break-in. **Insurance** is a deal where many people each pay a small, regular amount so that the few who have bad luck get help with the big bill. You trade a cost you can plan for against one you cannot.

## Sharing the risk

Picture 1,000 renters in one city. Each pays an insurance company $180 a year. In a typical year, about 20 of them have a theft or a fire and need $6,000 each to replace their things. Nobody knows in advance which 20 it will be.

```ledger One year for 1,000 renters
~ Each renter pays | $180 a year
Premiums collected: 1,000 × $180 | $180,000.00
- Claims paid: 20 × $6,000 | $120,000.00
= Left for the insurer's costs and profit | $60,000.00
```

Most of those renters will "lose" their $180 because nothing happened to them. That is the point: they bought protection, not a savings plan. The 20 who had a loss got $6,000 each, which they may not have been able to pay alone.

## The words on every policy

- **Premium**: what you pay to have the insurance, monthly or yearly, whether or not you use it.
- **Deductible**: the amount you pay toward a covered loss before the insurance starts paying.
- **Coinsurance**: after the deductible, the percentage of the bill you still share. With 20% coinsurance, you pay 20% and the insurer pays 80%. Some plans use a **copay** instead, a flat fee like $30 for a doctor visit.
- **Out-of-pocket maximum**: the most you will pay for covered care in a year (mostly in health insurance). After you reach it, the plan pays 100% of covered costs for the rest of the year.

## Following one bill

Aaliyah, 19, breaks her wrist. Her health plan has a $1,500 deductible, 20% coinsurance, and a $6,000 out-of-pocket maximum. The covered bill comes to $4,000, and she has not paid anything toward her deductible yet this year.

```ledger Who pays Aaliyah's $4,000 bill
Covered bill | $4,000.00
- Aaliyah pays her deductible | $1,500.00
= Left after the deductible | $2,500.00
- Aaliyah pays 20% coinsurance on $2,500 | $500.00
= The insurance plan pays | $2,000.00
```

```ledger Aaliyah's share
Deductible | $1,500.00
+ Coinsurance | $500.00
= Aaliyah pays in total | $2,000.00
# Under her $6,000 out-of-pocket maximum, so the cap did not kick in.
```

If the bill had been $50,000, Aaliyah would have paid $6,000, her out-of-pocket maximum, and the plan would have paid the rest. That cap is what protects you from a cost that could wipe out years of savings.

> [!tip] A higher deductible usually means a lower premium. That trade only works if you have savings to cover the deductible when something happens.

The [NAIC](https://www.naic.org), the organization of state insurance regulators, has consumer guides for every kind of insurance.

```takeaways
- Insurance pools many small premiums to pay for the few big losses.
- The premium is the price; the deductible is what you pay first when you file a claim.
- Coinsurance splits the bill after the deductible; the out-of-pocket maximum caps your share.
- Pick a deductible you could pay from savings tomorrow.
```
