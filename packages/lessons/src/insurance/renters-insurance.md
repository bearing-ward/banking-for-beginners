When you move into your first apartment or dorm-style rental, it is easy to assume the building's insurance covers you. It does not. The landlord's policy covers the building. Your laptop, clothes, and bike are on you.

**Renters insurance** covers three main things:

- **Personal property**: your belongings, if they are stolen or damaged by things like fire, smoke, or a burst pipe. This often applies even when they are away from home, such as a bike stolen at work.
- **Liability**: if you accidentally hurt someone or damage their property, like a bathtub overflow that ruins the apartment below.
- **Additional living expenses**: a hotel and meals if your place becomes unlivable while it is repaired.

## Add up what you own

Most people underestimate the value of their stuff. Grace, 19, makes a quick list before buying a policy.

```ledger Grace's belongings, cost to replace
Laptop | $900.00
+ Phone | $700.00
+ Clothes and shoes | $1,200.00
+ Bike | $450.00
+ Gaming console and games | $500.00
+ Bed, desk, and kitchen things | $850.00
= Total to replace | $4,600.00
```

Replacing all of that at once would take most new renters months of pay. Grace gets a policy with $15,000 of personal property coverage and a $500 deductible for $14 a month. Prices depend on where you live and the coverage you pick, so get your own quotes.

## A claim, start to finish

Someone breaks into Grace's apartment and takes her laptop, console, and bike. She files a police report, then a claim with photos and receipts.

```ledger Grace's theft claim
Laptop | $900.00
+ Gaming console and games | $500.00
+ Bike | $450.00
= Value of stolen items | $1,850.00
- Grace's deductible | $500.00
= Insurance pays | $1,350.00
```

That payout assumes **replacement cost** coverage, which pays what it costs to buy the items new. Cheaper **actual cash value** coverage pays what the used items were worth, which can be much less for a two-year-old laptop. Ask which one a quote includes.

## Before you buy

- Roommates usually need their own policies. One person's policy rarely covers another's things.
- Floods and earthquakes are usually not covered; they need separate policies.
- Some landlords require renters insurance in the lease.
- Take a video of your room and belongings now, and keep receipts for big purchases. It makes a claim much easier.

The [NAIC](https://www.naic.org) has a home inventory guide and explains what renters policies cover.

```takeaways
- Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your belongings.
- Renters insurance covers your things, your liability, and temporary housing.
- Replacement cost coverage pays to buy new; actual cash value pays what used items were worth.
- Make an inventory of your stuff before you need it.
```

```quiz
? A fire damages Lucas's apartment building. Whose insurance pays to replace his clothes and laptop?
- The landlord's building policy
- No one; fire is never covered
+ Lucas's renters insurance, if he has it
> The landlord's policy covers the building. Only Lucas's own renters policy covers his belongings.

? Destiny's $1,200 TV is stolen. She has replacement cost coverage and a $250 deductible. What does she receive?
+ $950
- $1,200
- $250
> She pays the deductible, so the insurer pays $1,200 − $250 = $950.
```
