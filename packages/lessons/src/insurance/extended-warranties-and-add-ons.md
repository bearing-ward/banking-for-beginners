You are buying a $300 pair of earbuds and the screen asks: "Add 2-year protection for $49?" An **extended warranty**, or protection plan, is a kind of insurance on one product. Stores offer them because they are profitable: on average, buyers pay more for the plans than the plans pay out in repairs.

That does not mean a plan is never worth it. It means you should check before you tap "Add."

## Ask four questions

1. **What is already covered?** Most new electronics come with a manufacturer's warranty, often one year. Some credit cards add extra warranty time. A plan that overlaps those years is paying for coverage you already have.
2. **What does it actually cover?** Many plans exclude accidents, loss, or theft, or charge a deductible per claim. Read the terms, not the ad.
3. **What would replacing it cost you?** If you could replace the item from savings without real hardship, you are your own insurance.
4. **How likely are you to need it?** A phone that goes everywhere with you breaks more often than a printer that sits on a desk.

## A phone plan, by the numbers

Imani, 17, is offered a phone protection plan for $15 a month. Each screen repair has a $29 deductible. Without the plan, a screen repair at an authorized shop costs $279. She plans to keep the phone two years.

```ledger Two years of the plan, with one screen repair
Plan: 24 months × $15 | $360.00
+ Deductible for one screen repair | $29.00
= Total cost with the plan | $389.00
```

```ledger Two years without the plan, with one screen repair
Screen repair at an authorized shop | $279.00
= Total cost without the plan | $279.00
```

With one repair, the plan costs Imani $110 more. With two cracked screens, it would come out ahead. If she instead put the $15 a month into savings, she would have $360 set aside after two years, and keep whatever she did not spend on repairs.

## Other add-ons you will meet

```table Common add-ons and what to check
| Add-on | Offered when | Check first |
| Rental car damage coverage | Renting a car | Your own auto policy or credit card may already cover it |
| Event ticket insurance | Buying concert tickets | What reasons for missing the event it actually covers |
| GAP insurance | Financing a car | Whether your loan could be larger than the car's value; compare the dealer's price with your insurer's |
| Electronics protection | Buying a phone, laptop, or TV | The manufacturer's warranty and what the plan excludes |
```

You can almost always buy a protection plan later, within a set window, so you can say "not today" at the register and look up the terms at home. The [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties) explains warranties and service contracts, and the [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov) covers add-ons sold with car loans.

> [!tip] An emergency fund works like a protection plan for everything you own, and you keep the money you do not use.

```takeaways
- Extended warranties are insurance on a single product and usually cost more than they pay out on average.
- Check what the manufacturer's warranty and your credit card already cover.
- Read what is excluded and whether there is a deductible.
- If you can afford to replace it, saving the premium is often the better deal.
```
