One trip to the emergency room can cost thousands of dollars. **Health insurance** pays a large share of medical costs in exchange for a monthly premium. Many teens are covered through a parent's job or a government program such as Medicaid or CHIP. Knowing how that coverage works now makes it easier to manage on your own later.

## Staying on a parent's plan until 26

Under federal law, if a parent's plan covers children, you can usually stay on it until you turn 26. That holds even if you move out, go to college, get married, or have a job that offers its own insurance. Ask your parent for a copy of the insurance card, or a photo of it, and keep it on your phone.

## In network and out of network

Each plan has a **network**: the doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies that agreed to lower prices with that insurer. Going **in network** costs you much less. Going **out of network** can mean the plan pays less or nothing, and the provider can bill you for the difference.

Zoe, 18, needs an MRI for a knee injury. She has not met her deductible yet this year, so she pays the full price of the scan either way. The difference is which price.

```ledger MRI at an in-network imaging center
Provider's full price | $2,400.00
- Discount the insurer negotiated | $1,500.00
= Zoe pays toward her deductible | $900.00
```

```ledger MRI at an out-of-network imaging center
Provider's full price | $2,400.00
- Negotiated discount | $0.00
= Zoe pays, and it may not count toward her deductible | $2,400.00
```

Before a planned visit, check that the provider is in network using your insurer's website or the number on your card. Ask the provider's office too.

> [!note] Federal law protects you from most surprise bills for emergency care, and for out-of-network doctors you did not choose at an in-network hospital. Learn more at [CMS.gov](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises).

## Common costs on a health plan

```table What you might pay for a few common visits
| | Typical cost-sharing |
| Preventive checkup or vaccine | Often $0 in network |
| Primary care visit | A copay, such as $30 |
| Urgent care | A copay, such as $75 |
| Emergency room | Deductible and coinsurance, up to your out-of-pocket maximum |
| Generic prescription | A small copay, such as $10 |
```

The amounts above are examples. Your plan's **Summary of Benefits and Coverage**, a standard form every plan must provide, lists your real costs.

## Getting your own plan

At some point you may need your own coverage: when you turn 26, or earlier if you are not on a parent's plan. Common options are a plan from an employer, Medicaid if your income is low, or a plan from the **Health Insurance Marketplace** at [HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov) (some states run their own marketplace site). Depending on your income, you may qualify for savings that lower your premium.

You can sign up during **open enrollment**, usually in the late fall, or within a limited time after a life change such as losing other coverage or turning 26. Do not wait until you need care; mark the date.

```takeaways
- You can usually stay on a parent's health plan until 26.
- In-network care costs much less; check the network before planned visits.
- Federal law limits most surprise bills for emergency care.
- HealthCare.gov is where to compare plans when you need your own.
```
