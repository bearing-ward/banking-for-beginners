Splitting a pizza, paying back a friend for concert tickets, collecting money for a group gift: a **payment app**, also called a P2P (person to person) app, such as Venmo, Zelle, or Cash App, makes it fast. Fast cuts both ways. Once you send money, it is usually gone for good.

Many payment apps require you to be 18, or let teens use them only with a parent's account. Check the app's age rules before you sign up.

## Treat a payment like cash

Sending money in an app is like handing someone cash. If you send it to the wrong person or a scammer, there is often no way to undo it. Federal rules protect you if someone gets into your account without permission, but if you were tricked into sending the money yourself, your bank or the app may treat it as a payment you approved.

So the main rule is short: **only pay people you know and trust, in person.** Do not use a payment app to buy from strangers online or to pay a deposit on something you have not seen.

## App balance vs. bank balance

When someone pays you in some apps, the money sits in the **app balance** until you move it to your bank. That balance is held by the app company, which is not a bank. It may not be FDIC insured unless the company places it at a partner bank that offers coverage, and that can depend on conditions in the app's terms.

```ledger Leo's app after a group dinner
Paid back by Sam | $18.50
+ Paid back by Ari | $18.50
+ Paid back by Jordan | $22.00
= App balance | $59.00
- Transferred to his bank, free standard transfer | $59.00
= Left in the app | $0.00
# An instant transfer would have arrived faster for a small fee.
```

Move money out of the app to your insured bank account. Keep the app for moving money, not for storing it.

## Scams to know

Scammers love payment apps because money moves instantly. Watch for these:

```message A "mistake" payment
Channel: dm
From: @kenzie_r_2209
Hey!! I sent you $200 on accident, it was for my roommate's rent. Can you please send it back?? I'm freaking out
! Someone you do not know sent you money "by accident"
! The first payment may be from a stolen account and get reversed later, leaving you out $200
! Pressure and urgency
```

If a stranger sends you money by mistake, do not send it back yourself. Contact the app's support and let them reverse it.

Other common ones: a seller who wants payment in an app before you see a phone or concert ticket, a "prize" that requires a fee, and a caller pretending to be your bank who asks you to send money to yourself "to protect it."

## Safer settings

- Make your activity **private** so strangers cannot see who you pay.
- Turn on a **PIN or face lock** and two-factor sign-in.
- Double-check the name and photo before you hit send. Start with $1 if you are unsure.

If you get scammed, report it to the app and your bank right away, and at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). Learn more about avoiding scams at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/fraud/).
