When Priya went to open her first account, her mom asked, "Bank or credit union?" Priya did not know there was a difference. Both offer checking, savings, debit cards, and loans, and both keep your money safe. The difference is who owns them and how they set fees and rates.

## Banks

A **bank** is a business owned by shareholders, and it aims to make a profit. Banks range from huge national chains with branches and ATMs in every city to small local banks and online-only banks. Deposits at a bank are insured by the **FDIC**, a federal agency.

## Credit unions

A **credit union** is a nonprofit owned by its members, the people who keep money there. Profits go back to members as lower fees, lower loan rates, or higher savings rates. To join, you need a connection the credit union accepts: where you live, work, or go to school, or a family member who already belongs. Deposits at a credit union are insured by the **NCUA**, the federal agency for credit unions, with the same protection as FDIC insurance.

```table Bank or credit union?
| | Bank | Credit union |
| Owned by | Shareholders | Members |
| Who can join | Anyone | People with a qualifying connection |
| Fees and rates | Vary; online banks are often low-fee | Often lower fees and loan rates |
| Branches and ATMs | Large banks have many | Fewer, but many share ATM networks |
| Deposit insurance | FDIC | NCUA |
```

## How to choose

The label matters less than the account itself. Compare the actual accounts you are looking at:

- **Monthly fees**, and what it takes to avoid them
- **Minimum balance** to open or to keep the account
- **ATMs** near your home, school, and job, and fees for using other ATMs
- **Overdraft** rules and fees
- **Savings rate**
- A good **app** with mobile deposit and alerts

```ledger What fees cost Priya in a year
~ Account A | $5 monthly fee, waived with $300 balance
~ Priya's usual balance | about $150, so she pays the fee
Monthly fee, 12 months | $60.00
+ Out-of-network ATM fees, 2 a month at $3 | $72.00
= Yearly cost of Account A | $132.00
# Account B at a credit union near her school: no monthly fee and free ATMs nearby, so $0.
```

Small fees add up. An account with no monthly fee and free ATMs where you go every day is worth more than one with a nicer logo.

```takeaways
- Banks are for-profit businesses; credit unions are nonprofits owned by members.
- Both are federally insured: banks by the FDIC, credit unions by the NCUA.
- Compare fees, minimums, ATMs, and overdraft rules, not the name on the door.
```
