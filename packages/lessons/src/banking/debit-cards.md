A **debit card** is linked to your checking account. When you tap or swipe it, the money comes out of your account, usually within a day or two. You can only spend what is in the account, which makes it a good first card.

## Your PIN

Your **PIN** is a 4-digit code you use at ATMs and some checkouts. Pick one that is not your birthday or 1234, never write it on the card, and never tell anyone, including someone who says they work for your bank. Your bank will not ask for it.

## Holds and pending charges

Some purchases put a **hold** on your money before the final amount is known. A gas pump, a hotel, or a rental car may hold more than you end up paying. A restaurant may hold the bill before you add the tip. The hold lowers your **available balance**, the amount you can spend right now, until the real charge posts. That can take a few days.

```ledger Jayden fills up his car
Available balance before the gas station | $150.00
- Hold placed by the pump | $100.00
= Available balance while the hold is on | $50.00
# Jayden pumped $38.50 of gas, but the pump held $100 until the charge was final.
```

```ledger Two days later, the real charge posts
Available balance before the gas station | $150.00
- Actual gas purchase | $38.50
= Available balance after it posts | $111.50
```

For two days, Jayden's app showed $61.50 less than he really had. If he had tried to spend that money, a purchase could have been declined or pushed him into an overdraft. When you use your card at a pump, paying inside for a set amount avoids a large hold.

## Debit vs. credit when something goes wrong

If someone steals your card number, the protection you get depends on the card type and how fast you report it.

```table If your card is lost or stolen
| | Debit card | Credit card |
| Whose money is gone | Yours, out of your account | The bank's, until you pay the bill |
| Most you can lose | $50 if you report within 2 business days | $50 at most |
| Report later | Up to $500, or more after 60 days from your statement | Still $50 at most |
| While it is sorted out | You may wait for your money back | You do not pay the disputed charge |
```

With debit, the money leaves your account right away, and you may wait days or weeks to get it back. That is why speed matters. Many banks offer stronger protection than the law requires, but do not count on it.

## Habits that keep your card safe

- Turn on purchase alerts in your bank's app.
- Lock or freeze the card in the app the moment you cannot find it, then call the bank.
- Cover the keypad when you enter your PIN, and skip ATMs or pumps with loose or odd card readers.
- Check your transactions every week.

More on lost or stolen cards: [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-get-my-money-back-after-i-discover-an-unauthorized-transaction-or-money-missing-from-my-bank-account-en-1017/).

```takeaways
- A debit card spends money straight from your checking account.
- Holds can lower your available balance for days, especially at gas pumps and hotels.
- Report a lost card or strange charge right away; with debit, your own money is at stake.
```
