Marcus is 16 and starting at a pizza place next week. His manager wants his account details for **direct deposit**, which sends his pay straight into a bank account instead of handing him a paper check. He does not have an account yet. Opening one takes about half an hour if he brings the right things.

## If you are under 18

In most states you must be 18 to open a bank account on your own, because opening one means signing a contract, and minors usually cannot be held to one. Under 18, you have two common options:

- A **joint account** with a parent or guardian. You both own it, you both can use it, and they can see the activity. Many banks offer teen checking accounts set up this way, often for ages 13 to 17.
- A **custodial account**, where an adult manages money that legally belongs to you until you reach the age your state sets.

The age of adulthood is 18 in most states but higher in a few, so check with the bank.

## What to bring

Banks are required to verify who you are. Bring these, and bring your parent or guardian if you are under 18 (they will need their own ID).

```table What to bring
| You need | Examples |
| Photo ID | Driver's license, state ID card, passport; some teen accounts accept a school ID with a parent's ID |
| Social Security number | Your card or the number itself; some banks accept an ITIN |
| Address | Often on your ID; otherwise a piece of mail |
| Opening deposit | Cash, a check, or a transfer; sometimes $0, sometimes $25 or more |
```

## Questions to ask before you sign

Fees are where a "free" account gets expensive. Ask these out loud, or find them on the bank's fee schedule:

- Is there a **monthly fee**? How do I avoid it?
- Is there a **minimum balance** to open or to keep the account?
- Which **ATMs** are free, and what does it cost to use others?
- What happens if I spend more than I have? Is **overdraft** coverage on or off?
- Does a teen account change into a regular account at 18, and do the fees change then?

```ledger Marcus opens his account
Opening deposit, birthday cash | $50.00

```

## After you open it

Download the bank's app, turn on alerts for low balance and large purchases, and give your employer the **routing number** (which identifies the bank) and your **account number**. Both are in the app. Keep your account number private, the way you would a password.

Learn more about choosing an account at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/bank-accounts/).

```takeaways
- Under 18, you usually need a joint account with a parent or a custodial account.
- Bring photo ID, your Social Security number, proof of address, and an opening deposit.
- Before you sign, ask about monthly fees, minimums, ATM fees, and overdraft.
```
