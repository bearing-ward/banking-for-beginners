Diego got his first paycheck from the grocery store and cashed it. A week later, the $240 in his wallet was down to $35, and he could not say where most of it went. Cash is easy to spend and easy to lose. A bank account keeps your money safe, gives you a record of every dollar, and lets you get paid by direct deposit.

Most people start with two accounts at the same bank: one for spending and one for saving.

```table Checking or savings?
| | Checking | Savings |
| Made for | Everyday spending | Money you are not spending yet |
| How you pay | Debit card, app transfers, checks | Move it to checking first |
| Interest | Little or none | Some, and more at some banks |
| Withdrawals | As many as you need | Some banks limit them each month |
```

## Checking: your spending account

A **checking account** is where your paycheck lands and where you spend from. It comes with a **debit card** that takes money straight out of the account when you buy something, and you can use it at ATMs. You can also pay bills, send money, and write checks from it.

Because money moves in and out of checking all the time, it usually earns little or no interest.

## Savings: your not-yet account

A **savings account** holds money you plan to keep for a while: an emergency cushion, a car, next year's prom. It pays **interest**, which is money the bank pays you for keeping your money there. Rates vary a lot between banks, so it is worth comparing.

Savings is a little harder to spend from on purpose. You usually move money to checking before you can spend it, and that small step makes impulse buys less likely.

> [!note] Some banks limit how many withdrawals or transfers you can make from savings each month, and may charge a fee or convert the account if you go over. Ask when you open it.

## Using both together

A simple setup: your paycheck goes into checking, and a set amount moves to savings on payday, before you can spend it. Many banks let you split direct deposit between accounts or schedule an automatic transfer.

```ledger Diego's payday split
Paycheck deposited to checking | $240.00
- Automatic transfer to savings | $40.00
= Left in checking to spend | $200.00
# Diego chose to move $40 each payday. The amount is up to you.
```

Keep enough in checking to cover what you will spend before the next payday, and let savings grow on its own.

```takeaways
- Checking is for spending; savings is for money you want to keep.
- Savings pays more interest and is a little harder to spend from, which helps.
- Moving money to savings on payday, before you spend, is the easiest way to save.
```
