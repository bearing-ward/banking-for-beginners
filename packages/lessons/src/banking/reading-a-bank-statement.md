Every month your bank sends a **statement**, a summary of everything that went in and out of your account. Most people never open it. Reading it takes five minutes and is how you catch fees, mistakes, and fraud.

A statement covers a set period, usually a month, and has four main numbers.

```ledger Sofia's checking statement, March 1 to 31
Beginning balance, March 1 | $212.40
+ Deposit: Payroll, Main St. Cafe | $264.18
+ Deposit: Payroll, Main St. Cafe | $281.06
- Debit card: Grocery store | $23.47
- Debit card: Movie theater | $14.50
- Autopay: Phone plan | $40.00
- Transfer to savings | $100.00
- ATM withdrawal, out of network | $40.00
- ATM fee | $3.00
= Ending balance, March 31 | $536.67
```

## The four numbers

- **Beginning balance**: what was in the account on the first day.
- **Deposits and credits**: money that came in, such as paychecks and transfers.
- **Withdrawals and debits**: money that went out, such as card purchases, ATM cash, bills, and fees.
- **Ending balance**: beginning balance plus deposits minus withdrawals.

Sofia's statement shows something she missed: a $3.00 fee for using an ATM outside her bank's network. That is easy to avoid next time.

## Pending and posted

In your bank's app, new transactions often show as **pending** first. That means the purchase happened but has not fully gone through. Once it **posts**, it is final and shows on your statement. A pending amount can change, for example when a restaurant adds your tip.

```table Pending or posted?
| | Pending | Posted |
| Has it happened? | Yes, but not final | Yes, and final |
| Can the amount change? | Sometimes | No |
| On your statement? | Not yet | Yes |
| Lowers your available balance? | Yes | Yes |
```

## What to look for

Go down the list and ask of each line: "Did I do this?"

- Charges you do not recognize, even small ones. Scammers often test a stolen card with a $1 charge.
- Fees: monthly, ATM, overdraft, paper statement.
- Subscriptions you forgot about or meant to cancel.
- Paychecks that are missing or smaller than your pay stub says.

If something is wrong, contact your bank right away. For electronic transactions, you generally have 60 days after the statement is sent to report an error and keep your full legal protection. Learn more at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/bank-accounts/).

```takeaways
- A statement shows beginning balance, money in, money out, and ending balance.
- Pending transactions can still change; posted ones are final.
- Read every line once a month and report anything you do not recognize quickly.
```
