title: Keeping your money safe with FDIC insurance

summary: Why money in an insured bank or credit union is safe even if the bank fails, and what insurance does not cover.
---

Banks do not keep your deposit in a vault. They lend most of it out and keep enough on hand for withdrawals. Once in a while, a bank fails. If that happens to yours, you do not lose your savings, as long as the bank is insured.

## What deposit insurance does

The **FDIC** (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation) is a federal agency that insures deposits at member banks. If an insured bank fails, the FDIC pays depositors back, usually within a few days, often by moving accounts to another bank. Since the FDIC started in 1934, no depositor has lost a penny of insured money.

Credit unions have the same protection through the **NCUA** (National Credit Union Administration).

In 2026, the coverage is **$250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category**. An **ownership category** is how the account is owned: in your name alone, jointly with someone else, in certain retirement accounts, and a few others. Each category is insured separately.

```ledger Nia's accounts at one insured bank
Checking, in her name | $640.00
+ Savings, in her name | $2,300.00
+ CD, in her name | $1,000.00
= Total in her single-owner accounts | $3,940.00
~ Insurance limit for that category | $250,000
# All of Nia's money is covered, with a lot of room to spare.
```

Most people will never come close to the limit. If you ever do, you can spread money across different insured banks or ownership categories.

## What is covered and what is not

Insurance covers deposits. It does not cover investments, even ones you buy through your bank, and it does not protect money held by companies that are not banks.

```table Is it FDIC or NCUA insured?
| | Insured | Not insured |
| Checking and savings | Yes | |
| Money market deposit accounts | Yes | |
| CDs | Yes | |
| Stocks, bonds, and mutual funds | | No, their value can go up and down |
| Crypto | | No |
| Cash in a payment app or brokerage | | Not always; check the company's terms |
| Items in a safe deposit box | | No |
```

## Check before you deposit

Insured banks show the FDIC sign at branches and on their websites. Do not rely on a logo alone; scam sites copy them. Look up any bank on the FDIC's [BankFind tool](https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/bankfind), and read more about coverage at [fdic.gov](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance).

Insurance protects you when a bank fails. It does not protect you from a scam where you send money yourself, or from someone using your password. Those protections come from your own habits.

```takeaways
- In 2026, FDIC and NCUA insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category.
- It covers deposits like checking, savings, and CDs, not investments or crypto.
- Confirm a bank is insured with the FDIC's BankFind before you put money there.
```
