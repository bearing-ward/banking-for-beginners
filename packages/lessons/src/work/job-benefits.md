Priya has two offers for a part-time job she plans to keep all year, about 20 hours a week. The bookstore pays $16.00 an hour. The grocery store pays $15.00, but it offers paid time off and an employee discount. The higher wage looks like the obvious pick, but the hourly rate is only part of what a job pays.

**Benefits** are anything of value your employer gives you besides your wage. Part-time and first jobs often have few benefits, but many offer some, and full-time jobs later will offer more. Learning to count them now helps you compare offers for the rest of your working life.

## Benefits you may see at a first job

- **Paid time off** (PTO): hours you get paid for when you are sick or on vacation. Some states and cities require paid sick time.
- **Employee discounts**: money off what the store sells.
- **Free meals or uniforms**: common at restaurants and theme parks.
- **Schedule flexibility**: not money, but worth a lot when you have school and sports.

```ledger What the grocery store's extras are worth in a year
~ Hours of paid time off | 40 × $15.00
Paid time off | $600.00
~ Employee discount | 20% off $300 of groceries
+ Discount savings | $60.00
= Value of extras | $660.00
```

Over a year of about 1,000 hours (20 hours a week for 50 weeks), the bookstore's extra $1.00 an hour is worth $1,000, which still beats $660 of extras. Priya might choose the grocery store anyway if the schedule works better, and that is a fair reason. The point is to compare with numbers, not guesses.

## Benefits you will see at a full-time job

**Health insurance** helps pay for doctor visits, prescriptions, and emergencies. Employers usually pay much of the cost, and your share comes out of each paycheck as a **premium**. You can learn the terms at [healthcare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov).

A **401(k)** is a retirement account through your job. You choose a percentage of each paycheck to put in. In a traditional 401(k), it goes in before income tax is figured, which lowers your income tax now. Social Security and Medicare still apply to that money. In 2026, you can put in up to $24,500 a year.

Many employers add an **employer match**: money they put in when you do. A match is part of your pay that you only get if you contribute, so it is worth capturing.

```ledger A first full-time job, one year of 401(k) saving
~ Salary | $40,000.00
~ Plan rule | Employer matches 100% of the first 5% you put in
Your contribution, 5% of $40,000 | $2,000.00
+ Employer match | $2,000.00
= Into your 401(k) this year | $4,000.00
```

> [!note] Some matches **vest** over time, which means you must stay a certain number of years to keep the employer's part. Your own contributions are always yours. Investments go up and down, so the balance will too. See the [IRS 401(k) page](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/401k-plans) for the basics.

## Compare offers side by side

When you get an offer, ask what benefits come with it, and when they start. Then put the offers in a table.

```table Priya's two offers
| | Bookstore | Grocery store |
| Hourly wage | $16.00 | $15.00 |
| Paid time off | None | 40 hours a year |
| Employee discount | None | 20% |
| Schedule | Fixed weekday shifts | Flexible, weekends optional |
```

```takeaways
- Benefits are part of your pay: PTO, discounts, health insurance, and retirement matches.
- Turn benefits into dollars so you can compare offers fairly.
- An employer 401(k) match is extra pay you only get if you contribute; in 2026 you can put in up to $24,500.
- Ask what benefits come with a job, and when they start.
```

```quiz
? Your employer matches 401(k) contributions up to 5% of pay. What happens if you put in nothing?
- You get the match anyway
+ You get no match
- The match goes into your paycheck instead
> A match only arrives when you contribute. Skipping it leaves part of your pay on the table.

? Which is a benefit?
- Your hourly wage
- Social Security withheld from your check
+ Paid time off
> Benefits are value beyond your wage. Social Security withholding is a tax, not a benefit from your employer.

? Job A pays $1.00 an hour more. Job B offers $660 a year in extras. You will work 400 hours this year. Which pays more in total?
- Job A
+ Job B
- They are the same
> At 400 hours, Job A's extra dollar is worth $400, less than Job B's $660 of extras.
```
