Leah got the job at the ice cream shop. Before her first shift, the manager hands her a folder of forms. They look official and a little scary, but there are only a few, and each one has a clear job.

Bring your documents and your bank information on your first day, and fill the forms out carefully. A typo in your name or Social Security number can delay your pay or mess up your tax forms later.

## Your Social Security number

Your **Social Security number** (SSN) is the nine-digit number the government uses to track your earnings and taxes. Your employer needs it to report your pay. If you do not know where your card is, ask a parent. If it is lost, you can request a replacement from the [Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/number-card) for free.

```table The forms in your first-day folder
| Form | What it does | What you need |
| Form I-9 | Proves you are allowed to work in the US | Identity and work documents |
| Form W-4 | Tells your employer how much federal income tax to withhold | Your SSN and filing status |
| Direct deposit form | Sends your pay straight to your bank account | Routing and account numbers |
| State forms | State tax withholding or a work permit, in some states | Depends on your state |
```

## Form I-9

The **Form I-9** shows that you are who you say you are and that you can legally work in the US. You fill out the first section by your first day, then show original documents within your first few days. A US passport covers everything on its own. Otherwise, you show one document for identity, such as a driver's license or a school ID with your photo, plus one that shows you can work, such as your Social Security card or a birth certificate. Workers under 18 have a few extra options. The full list is on the form, which you can preview at [uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9).

```table Common ways teens complete the I-9
| Option | Show this | Plus this |
| One document | US passport | Nothing else |
| Two documents | Driver's license or state ID | Social Security card |
| Two documents | School ID with your photo | Birth certificate |
```

## Form W-4

The **Form W-4** tells your employer how much **federal income tax** to take out of each paycheck. For most teens with one job, it is short: fill in Step 1 (your name, address, SSN, and filing status, usually "Single") and sign Step 5. The other steps are for people with more than one job, children, or other income. The lesson on the W-4 and withholding goes deeper. Your state may have its own version.

## Direct deposit

**Direct deposit** sends your pay straight into your bank account, usually faster and safer than a paper check. The form asks for your bank's **routing number** (which identifies the bank) and your **account number** (which identifies your account). You can find both in your banking app or on a check. Double-check every digit.

## Protect your number

Your SSN is the key to your identity. Someone who has it can open accounts or file taxes in your name. Give it to an employer once you are hired, on their official forms. Do not carry your card in your wallet, and do not send your number by text, email, or social media DM.

```message A "job offer" that wants your SSN
Channel: text
From: +1 (555) 014-7781
Congrats! You were selected for a remote data entry job, $28/hr. To start today, reply with your full name, date of birth, and Social Security number for payroll.
! You never applied or interviewed
! Asks for your SSN by text
! Pay that sounds too good for no experience
```

> [!tip] If you think someone has your SSN and is misusing it, go to [identitytheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov) for a step-by-step recovery plan.

```takeaways
- The I-9 proves you can work; bring original documents in your first days.
- The W-4 sets your federal income tax withholding; most first-time workers fill in Step 1 and sign Step 5.
- Direct deposit needs your routing and account numbers; check every digit.
- Share your SSN only on official forms after you are hired, never by text or DM.
```
