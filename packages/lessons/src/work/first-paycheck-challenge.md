summary: Match a deposit to your pay stub, account for spending, and make a plan before the next payday.
---

You have read a pay stub and a bank statement. Now use both. All amounts in this practice story are fictional. The pay stub already shows deductions; you do not need to estimate taxes.

## Try it before reading the answer

Nia, 17, has $45 in checking before payday. Her pay stub shows $300 gross pay, $25 in deductions, and $275 net pay. Her bank then lists a $275 payroll deposit, a $20 bus pass, and a $30 grocery purchase. Everything has posted, and nothing else is pending.

Work out her ending balance. Does the deposit match the stub? Then decide how much she can put toward a laptop after reserving $40 for her phone bill, $50 for food and travel before payday, and a $30 checking buffer.

```table Nia's records
| Record | Amount | What to compare |
| Stub gross pay | $300 | Before deductions |
| Stub deductions | $25 | Taken out before deposit |
| Stub net pay | $275 | Expected payroll deposit |
| Bank payroll deposit | $275 | Matches net pay |
```

## Check the account first

The deposit matches net pay. Gross pay is not available to budget because the deductions have already come out. **Reconciling** means comparing your records and explaining the difference between balances.

```ledger Nia's posted checking transactions
Starting balance | $45.00
+ Payroll deposit | $275.00
- Bus pass | $20.00
- Groceries | $30.00
= Ending balance | $270.00
```

If your deposit does not match, compare the pay period and deposit date before assuming money is missing. Check for split deposits too. Ask payroll about an unexplained difference and keep copies of the records. The [OCC guide to checking accounts](https://occ.gov/topics/consumers-and-communities/consumer-protection/depository-services/checking-accounts.html) explains why understanding the balance matters.

## Give the remaining money a job

Nia's app shows $270, but she cannot spend all of it on the laptop. Money reserved for upcoming costs is still in her balance until she pays those costs.

```ledger Nia's plan through next payday
Ending checking balance | $270.00
- Phone bill reserve | $40.00
- Food and travel reserve | $50.00
- Checking buffer | $30.00
= Available for laptop savings | $150.00
```

Her next action is to reserve the bill money, move $150 to laptop savings, and check the account again before buying anything else. If a new cost appears, revise the plan rather than counting the same dollars twice.

```takeaways
- Compare a payroll deposit with net pay for the same pay period.
- Reconcile the account before planning what to spend.
- Subtract upcoming costs and a buffer before moving money to a goal.
```
