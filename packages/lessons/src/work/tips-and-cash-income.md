Jada works weekends as a barista. Customers tip on the card reader, and some drop cash in the jar. By Sunday night her apron pocket has $40 in it, and it is easy to think of that money as a bonus that does not count. It does count. Tips are **income**, the same as your hourly pay.

## Tips are taxable

**Tips** are money customers give you on top of the price: on a card, in cash, or through a payment app. Federal tax law treats them as part of your pay, so Social Security and Medicare apply to them, and they count toward income tax.

**Card tips** usually show up on your pay stub automatically. **Cash tips** do not. Your employer only knows about them if you tell them. If you get cash tips at a job, you generally must report them to your employer every month, usually through an app or a short form. The IRS explains the rules in [Tax Topic 761](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc761).

```ledger Jada's weekend pay, everything counted
~ Hours worked | 25 × $12.00
Wages | $300.00
+ Card tips | $85.00
+ Cash tips she reported | $40.00
= Total pay | $425.00
```

## What comes out

Once tips are reported, your employer takes Social Security and Medicare out of your paycheck on the full amount, including the cash you already have in your pocket. That can make a paycheck look small, but the math is the same as always.

```ledger FICA on Jada's week
~ Total pay | $425.00
Social Security, 6.2% of $425 | $26.35
+ Medicare, 1.45% of $425 | $6.16
= Total FICA | $32.51
```

## Why reporting helps you

Reporting tips can feel like paying more for nothing, but it builds your future. Social Security tracks your lifetime earnings to decide your retirement and disability benefits, and unreported cash never makes it into that record. Reported tips also count when you apply for an apartment, a car loan, or a credit card and need to show your income.

Starting with 2025, there is also a new federal deduction that lets many workers in tipped jobs subtract some of their tips when they figure their federal income tax. It does not change Social Security or Medicare, and you still report every tip. Details are on the [IRS page about the new deductions](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/working-families-tax-cuts-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors). States have their own rules.

## Cash from side jobs

Money from mowing lawns, pet sitting, or tutoring is income too, even if a neighbor pays you in cash or through a payment app and nobody sends you a tax form. This usually counts as **self-employment** income, which has its own rules (see the lesson on gig work).

The habit that makes all of this easy is a simple log: date, job, and amount.

```table Jada's tip log
| Date | Card tips | Cash tips |
| Saturday | $45.00 | $22.00 |
| Sunday | $40.00 | $18.00 |
| Weekend total | $85.00 | $40.00 |
```

> [!tip] Keep the log in a notes app or spreadsheet and update it at the end of every shift. It takes a minute and makes tip reports and tax time painless.

```takeaways
- Tips and cash from side jobs are income, even with no form.
- Report cash tips to your employer every month; card tips usually show up automatically.
- Reported tips count toward your Social Security record and prove your income later.
- Keep a simple log of the date, job, and amount.
```
