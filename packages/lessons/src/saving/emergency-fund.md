Ella is 17. She drives to her job at a garden center and pays for her own phone, gas, and part of her car insurance. One morning her car needs a $340 repair. If she had nothing saved, she would have to borrow or put it on a credit card. She had an **emergency fund**, so she paid it and drove to work.

An emergency fund is money set aside only for real surprises: things you did not plan for and cannot skip.

## What counts as an emergency

```table Emergency or not?
| Emergency | Not an emergency |
| Car repair you need to get to work | A concert that went on sale today |
| Replacing a broken phone you rely on | A newer phone model |
| A medical or dental bill | Holiday gifts (you know they are coming) |
| Losing hours at work | A sale on sneakers |
```

Things you know are coming, like gifts, prom, or car registration, are not emergencies. Plan for them with goal savings instead.

## How much to save

A common goal for adults is three to six months of **must-pay costs**: the bills you would have to pay even if your income stopped. For a teen living at home, two stages make sense:

1. **A starter fund** of $500. That covers most small surprises.
2. **A full fund** of three months of your own must-pay costs.

```ledger Ella's must-pay costs each month
Phone plan | $45.00
+ Her share of car insurance | $110.00
+ Gas for work | $80.00
= Must-pay costs per month | $235.00
```

```ledger Ella's full emergency fund goal
~ Months to cover | 3
Month 1 | $235.00
+ Month 2 | $235.00
+ Month 3 | $235.00
= Goal | $705.00
- Already saved | $200.00
= Still to save | $505.00
# At $60 a month, Ella needs 9 months. After 8 months she has added $480, still $25 short.
```

Try the planner with your own numbers. If the goal feels huge, start with the starter fund.

## Where to keep it

Keep your emergency fund in a **savings account** you can reach within a day or two. Not in your checking account, where it gets spent. Not invested in stocks, which can be down right when you need the money. A savings account at an FDIC-insured bank or NCUA-insured credit union is safe and earns some interest.

## Use it, then refill it

When a real emergency happens, use the fund. That is what it is for. Then make refilling it your next saving goal.

```takeaways
- An emergency fund is for real surprises, not for things you know are coming.
- Start with $500, then build toward three months of your must-pay costs.
- Keep it in a separate insured savings account, and refill it after you use it.
```
