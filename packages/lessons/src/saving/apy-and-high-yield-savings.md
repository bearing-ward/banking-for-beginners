Mateo has $2,000 saved from his summer job at a car wash. It sits in a savings account paying 0.01%. His cousin's account pays about 4%. Same amount of money, very different results.

## Interest rate vs. APY

The **interest rate** is the yearly rate before compounding. The **APY** (annual percentage yield) is what you actually earn in a year once compounding is included. Because interest earns interest, the APY is a little higher than the rate whenever interest compounds more than once a year.

```table Same rate, different compounding
| Interest rate | Compounded | APY |
| 4.00% | Once a year | 4.00% |
| 4.00% | Monthly | 4.07% |
| 4.00% | Daily | 4.08% |
```

By federal law, banks and credit unions have to show the APY on savings accounts, so you can compare accounts fairly. When you compare, compare APY to APY.

## What the difference is worth

```ledger $2,000 for one year at 0.01% APY
Deposit | $2,000.00
+ Interest for the year | $0.20
= Balance after one year | $2,000.20
```

```ledger $2,000 for one year at 4.07% APY
~ 4.00% interest rate, compounded monthly
Deposit | $2,000.00
+ Interest for the year | $81.48
= Balance after one year | $2,081.48
```

Mateo would earn $81.28 more in one year by moving his money, for about ten minutes of work.

## High-yield savings accounts

A **high-yield savings account** is a savings account with an APY well above the typical rate. They are often at online banks or credit unions, which have lower costs and pass some of the savings on. Rates in this lesson are examples; real rates change with the economy.

Before you open one, check:

- **Insurance.** Confirm the bank is FDIC insured or the credit union is NCUA insured.
- **Fees and minimums.** A monthly fee can wipe out the extra interest.
- **The rate can change.** Savings APYs are **variable**, meaning the bank can raise or lower them any time. A high rate that only lasts a few months is called a **teaser rate**.
- **Transfer time.** Moving money to your checking account at another bank can take one to three business days. That is fine for savings, and even helps you not spend it.

## Make the switch

You can keep your checking account where it is and open a high-yield savings account somewhere else, then link the two. Set your payday transfer to go to the new account.

```takeaways
- APY includes compounding, so it is the number to compare between accounts.
- On $2,000, the gap between a near-zero rate and a high-yield rate can be about $80 a year.
- Check insurance, fees, and whether the rate is variable or a teaser before you switch.
```
