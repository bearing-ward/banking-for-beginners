title: Why save, and how to start
level: starter
summary: Saving turns money you earn now into choices you have later. The trick is to save first, before you spend.
---

Liam babysits for two families and makes about $150 a week. By Sunday it is usually gone: food with friends, a game, gas money. Nothing he bought was a bad choice. But when his phone screen cracked, he had nothing set aside and had to borrow from his sister.

**Saving** means keeping part of what you earn for later instead of spending it now. It is what lets you handle a surprise, buy something big without borrowing, and say yes to an opportunity when it shows up.

## What saving does for you

- **Covers surprises.** A cracked screen, a flat tire, or a lost charger stops being a crisis.
- **Pays for big things.** A car, a laptop, a trip, a first apartment deposit.
- **Keeps you out of debt.** Money you have saved does not charge interest. Money you borrow does.
- **Gives you options.** Savings lets you take a lower-paying internship you are excited about, or quit a job that is not working.

## Pay yourself first

Most people save whatever is left at the end of the week, and usually nothing is left. Flip it: on payday, move your savings out first, then spend the rest. This is called **paying yourself first**.

```ledger Liam pays himself first
Weekly babysitting pay | $150.00
- Moved to savings on payday | $30.00
= Left to spend this week | $120.00
# Liam saves 20% of what he earns. Start with any amount you can keep up.
```

The amount matters less than the habit. Even $10 a week adds up, and you can raise it when your pay goes up.

```table What a weekly habit adds up to in a year
| Saved each week | After 52 weeks |
| $10 | $520 |
| $20 | $1,040 |
| $30 | $1,560 |
| $50 | $2,600 |
```

## Make it automatic

Willpower runs out. Automation does not. Set up an automatic transfer from checking to savings on each payday, or ask your employer to split your direct deposit so part goes straight to savings. If you are paid in cash, deposit the savings part first.

Keep your savings in a separate account from your spending money, so it does not quietly get spent. Your next steps: build an emergency fund, then save for specific goals.

```takeaways
- Saving gives you a cushion for surprises and a way to buy big things without debt.
- Pay yourself first: move savings out on payday, then spend what is left.
- Automate it, and keep savings in a separate account.
```
