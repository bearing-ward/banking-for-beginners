title: Privacy and sharing online
level: starter
summary: App permissions and audience settings can limit what you share, but screenshots and public profiles can spread details beyond your control. Here are ways to protect your privacy and get help.
---

Posting a team photo or a story from a school event can feel like sharing with friends. But a **profile audience** is the group allowed to see a post, and that group may be wider than you expect. A public profile can show posts to people you do not know. Someone can save or screenshot a post, even if you later delete it or set your account to private. Privacy settings help limit sharing; they cannot guarantee control over a copy.

## Check what an app can access

An **app permission** lets an app use something on your device, such as your camera, contacts, microphone, or location. Think about whether the app needs that access for the feature you want. You can review permissions in your device settings and change them later. The [FTC explains how apps collect information and how to review permissions](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-websites-and-apps-collect-and-use-your-information).

```table Permission check before using an app
| App feature | Permission to review | Question to ask |
| Photo editor | Photos | Does it need access to every photo, or can I choose one? |
| School map | Location | Do I want location access only while using the app? |
| Group chat | Contacts | Can I add people without sharing my full contact list? |
| Voice recorder | Microphone | Can I turn access off when I am done? |
```

Location details can appear in a post, a photo, or a live map. Before posting a team picture, check for a school name, street sign, schedule, house number, or location tag. A post that seems harmless by itself can reveal a routine when combined with other public posts.

## Choose your audience and pause before posting

Before you post, check whether the account is public and who can view the post, tag you, or send you a message. A private account can reduce who sees your posts, but a follower can still screenshot or forward them. Ask before posting a picture that includes someone else. The [FTC's sharing guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/heads-up) recommends thinking about the audience and checking privacy and location settings.

```table What a setting can and cannot do
| Setting or choice | Can help with | Does not guarantee |
| Private account | Limiting who follows and sees posts | That followers will not save or share a post |
| Turn off location tag | Keeping a post from showing a tagged place | That signs or other details in the image hide the location |
| Limit app permission | Reducing what the app can access on your device | That the app has no information you shared earlier |
| Delete a post | Removing it from your profile | Removing screenshots or copies other people saved |
```

**Doxxing** means posting someone's identifying details, such as a home address or phone number, to expose or target them. If someone posts your details or threatens you, it is not your fault. Do not argue with the person or repost the details. Save the link or a screenshot if safe, report the post to the platform, and tell a trusted adult such as a parent, school counselor, or coach. If you think someone may come to you or your home, get to a safe place and ask an adult or local emergency services for help.

You can review one account today: check its audience, location sharing, and app permissions. Change any setting that does not fit how you use it, and ask a trusted adult for help if a post makes you feel unsafe.

```takeaways
- Review app permissions and ask whether each one is needed for the feature you use.
- Check post audiences and location details before sharing.
- Private settings lower exposure but cannot stop screenshots or forwarding.
- If someone shares your details to target you, tell a trusted adult and report the post; it is not your fault.
```
