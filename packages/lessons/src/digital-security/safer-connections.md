You may need to check a pay stub, bank balance, or school payment online. A **secure connection** helps protect information as it travels between your device and a website. It does not prove the website is honest or that the computer you are using is safe.

## HTTPS protects the trip, not the destination

Look for https:// at the start of the web address. HTTPS encrypts information sent between your browser and that website. It helps keep people on the same Wi-Fi network from reading the information in transit. But scammers can use HTTPS too. It does not prove that a site belongs to your bank or school. Type a known address yourself or use a saved official bookmark, and check the spelling before signing in. The [FTC explains what HTTPS does and does not show](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping).

```table What to check before signing in
| Check | What it tells you | What it does not tell you |
| Address starts with https:// | The connection to that site is encrypted | That the site is your real bank or school |
| Address spelling matches the official site | You may be at the right organization | That a message linking there was safe to open |
| Login page appears familiar | The page looks like one you have used | That it is not a copy made to steal a password |
```

If a link arrived in a surprise text or email, do not use it to reach your account. Open the official app or enter the address you already know. If the spelling or page feels off, stop and ask the organization through contact details on a statement or card.

## Check the Wi-Fi network

At a library or cafe, several networks may have similar names. Ask staff for the exact network name or check an official sign before joining. Avoid networks with names that look almost right, and turn off automatic joining if your device keeps connecting to unknown networks. If you cannot confirm a network, your phone's cellular connection is another option if your plan and signal allow it.

Public Wi-Fi is common, and websites often encrypt connections. You do not need to panic when you see a public network. Still, check that you joined the right one and use HTTPS for sign-ins. The [FTC's current public Wi-Fi guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know) explains that encryption has made public Wi-Fi use safer than it used to be.

## Be careful on a shared computer

A library computer or a friend's laptop may remember account information for the next person. Avoid signing in to a bank or payment account on a shared computer when you can use your own device. If you must use one, do not save your password or choose "remember me." When you finish, use the account's **log out** option. Closing the browser tab is not the same as logging out; the account session may stay open.

```table Before leaving a shared computer
| Step | What to do |
| Sign in | Do not save the password or choose "remember me" |
| Finish the task | Use the website's log out option |
| After logging out | Confirm the site shows you are signed out, then close the tab |
| If you saved a password by mistake | Remove the saved password; from your own trusted device, change it and revoke the shared device session |
```

A **VPN** (virtual private network) sends your internet traffic through a service that encrypts the connection between your device and that service. A VPN does not check whether a website is real, make a fake link safe, or protect information typed into a compromised computer. It is not a replacement for checking the website address and logging out.

Try these steps the next time you check a school or money account: confirm the network, check the site address, and log out when finished, especially on a shared device.

```takeaways
- HTTPS encrypts traffic to a website, but does not prove that the website is trustworthy.
- Confirm the exact Wi-Fi name; cellular service is an option when you cannot confirm it.
- On a shared computer, avoid financial logins when possible, never save credentials, and log out explicitly.
- A VPN does not make a scam website or unsafe computer trustworthy.
```
