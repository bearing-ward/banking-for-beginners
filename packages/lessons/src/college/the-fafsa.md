The **FAFSA** (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) is the form colleges and the government use to decide how much help you get paying for school. It is the key to federal grants, **work-study** (a part-time job through your school), and federal student loans. Most states and many colleges use it to hand out their own aid too, and some scholarships require it.

The first word is the most important: it is free. You file it at [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov), the U.S. Department of Education's site. Nobody should ever charge you to file it.

## Who should file

Everyone heading to college or career school should file, even if you think your family earns too much. There is no income cutoff for filing, many schools require it for their own scholarships, and it is the only way to get a federal student loan. You file a new FAFSA for every year you are in school.

## What you need

```table Before you start
| What | Why |
| A StudentAid.gov account | You sign the form with it; this is often called an FSA ID |
| An account for each contributor | Usually a parent, who also has to sign |
| Your Social Security number | It identifies you |
| Tax information from two years earlier | For fall 2027, that means 2025 taxes |
| Savings and investment balances | Some families report these |
| A list of colleges | Each one you list gets your results |
```

Make your StudentAid.gov account a few days before you plan to file, since it can take time to verify. Your parent or other **contributor** needs their own account with their own email; do not share one. The form can pull tax information straight from the IRS once each person gives permission.

## Deadlines

The FAFSA usually opens in the fall for the next school year. The federal deadline is late, at the end of the school year, but that is not the one that matters most. Many states and colleges have much earlier deadlines, and some states give out grants until the money runs out. Each state and school sets its own, so check your state's deadline on [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/apply-for-aid/fafsa/fafsa-deadlines) and each college's financial aid page, then file as early as you can.

## After you file

You get a **FAFSA Submission Summary** showing your **Student Aid Index** (SAI), a number schools use to figure how much aid you need. Each college you listed then sends a financial aid offer. If your family's situation has changed a lot since that tax year, such as a lost job, contact the college's financial aid office and ask about a review.

## Watch for fakes

```message An email about your FAFSA
Channel: email
From: support@fafsa-priority-processing.co
Subject: Final notice: your FAFSA is incomplete
Your student aid will be canceled unless you pay the $79 priority processing fee within 24 hours. Click here to complete payment.
! A fee for a free form
! A deadline meant to rush you
! A sender that is not StudentAid.gov
```

Never give your StudentAid.gov username and password to anyone, including a company that offers to "help."

```takeaways
- The FAFSA is free and filed at StudentAid.gov; never pay to file it.
- File every year, even if you think you will not qualify for aid.
- State and college deadlines are often much earlier than the federal one.
- You and each contributor need your own StudentAid.gov account.
```
