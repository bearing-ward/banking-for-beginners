A student loan can be a smart investment in yourself. It can also follow you for decades. The difference is usually how much you borrow compared with what you will earn.

## A rule of thumb

A common guideline: keep your **total** student loans for your whole degree below what you expect to earn in your **first year** after graduating. If you expect to start at $45,000 a year, try to borrow less than $45,000 in total.

To find a realistic starting salary, look up your career in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' [Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/), and look up what graduates of your major at your school earn on the [College Scorecard](https://collegescorecard.ed.gov). Use the lower end of the range for a first job.

## Turn the total into a monthly payment

A big total is hard to picture. A monthly payment is not. Marisol plans to be a teacher. Say she borrows $27,000 over four years, at an example rate of 6%, and repays it over 10 years.

```ledger Marisol's student loans, at an example 6%
~ Repayment | 120 months
~ Monthly payment | $299.76
Amount borrowed | $27,000.00
+ Interest over 10 years | $8,971.20
= Total repaid | $35,971.20
```

You can check this in the loan calculator beside this lesson: enter $27,000 as the price, $0 down, a 6% APR, and 120 months.

## How the total changes your life

Here is the same 10-year loan at different amounts, compared with a $45,000 salary, which is $3,750 a month before taxes.

```table 10-year repayment at an example 6%
| Total borrowed | Monthly payment | Share of $3,750 monthly pay | Total interest |
| $27,000 | $299.76 | 8% | $8,971.20 |
| $40,000 | $444.08 | 12% | $13,289.60 |
| $80,000 | $888.16 | 24% | $26,579.20 |
```

At $27,000, the payment fits next to rent, a car, and saving. At $80,000, almost a quarter of Marisol's pay goes to the loan before taxes, rent, or food.

Stretching repayment over more years lowers the payment but raises the cost. Repaying the same $27,000 over 20 years drops the payment to $193.44, but the interest grows to $19,425.60.

## Habits that keep debt down

- Borrow only for what school costs, not for spending money. You can accept less than you are offered.
- Use free money and federal loans before private ones.
- Keep a running total of what you have borrowed. Your federal loans are listed in your account at [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov).
- Finish your degree. Debt without a degree is the hardest kind to repay.
- Work part time or in the summer, and pay interest while in school if you can.

> [!note] Real student loan rates are set each year and depend on the kind of loan. The 6% in this lesson is only an example so the math is easy to follow.

```takeaways
- Keep total student loans below your expected first-year salary.
- Turn any loan total into a monthly payment before you sign.
- Longer repayment lowers the payment but raises total interest.
- Borrow only what school costs, and keep a running total.
```
