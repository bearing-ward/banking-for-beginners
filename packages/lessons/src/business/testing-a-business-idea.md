---
title: Test a business idea before spending
level: starter
summary: Find out whether someone wants what you can offer before buying equipment or a pile of supplies.
---

Maya wants to tutor younger students in math. A new tablet and paid ads sound like a serious start. First, she needs to know whether families want her help at a price and time she can offer.

**Demand** means people are willing and able to buy something. Friends saying "cool idea" is encouragement, not proof of demand. All prices and results below are hypothetical.

## Start with a customer and a problem

Ask people about what they do now, not whether they like you. The [SBA's market research guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis) suggests investigating demand, alternatives, and prices.

```table Maya's first questions for families she knows
| Question | What it helps her learn |
| Which math tasks have been difficult lately? | Whether there is a specific problem |
| What help have you tried? | What alternatives already exist |
| When could a student attend a session? | Whether schedules can match |
| Would you book this specific session at $15? | Whether interest turns into a real decision |
```

Maya compares library homework help, school tutoring, and other local tutors. A free option may solve the problem already. She can change the offer or decide not to proceed.

## Make the test small enough to stop

A **pilot** is a small trial before a larger launch. Maya sets a $12 spending limit and offers four half-hour sessions in a permitted location. She has the materials she needs already. She explains the topic, price, time, and cancellation terms before anyone books.

```table Maya's one-week pilot plan
| Decision | Plan made before the test |
| Customer | Families she knows with students learning fractions |
| Offer | One half-hour practice session for $15 |
| Limit | Four sessions, at most $12 spent |
| Evidence | Bookings, completed paid sessions, and feedback |
| Next step | Review the results before buying equipment |
```

Ask a trusted adult about safe arrangements and permission when working with younger students. Check school or library rules before advertising or using space. Local business requirements and rules for minors vary; consult your city or county business office before offering paid services.

## Count behavior and costs

Eight people say they like the idea. Three book and complete a paid session. Maya spends her full test budget. No one pays for an uncompleted session in this example.

```ledger Maya's hypothetical pilot result
Three completed sessions at $15 | $45.00
- Printing and practice supplies | $12.00
= Left before taxes and paying herself for time | $33.00
~ Preparation and teaching time | 3 hours total
~ Left per hour before taxes | $11.00
```

Three customers are useful evidence, not a promise of steady sales. Maya asks what helped and what should change. She also checks whether she wants to keep doing the preparation. The $33 is not the same as $33 of take-home pay.

She decides to try another small week using the same materials. If no one had booked, she could ask why, change one part of the offer, or stop without losing hundreds of dollars.

Before spending on your idea, write down one customer, one specific offer, a spending limit, and what result would make you continue or change the plan.

```takeaways
- Test a specific offer with possible customers before making a large purchase.
- Bookings and completed paid work provide stronger evidence than compliments.
- Limit the trial's cost and include your preparation time when judging results.
- A small test can help you decide to continue, revise, or stop.
```
