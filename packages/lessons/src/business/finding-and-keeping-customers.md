---
title: Finding and keeping customers
level: starter
summary: Make a clear offer, reach people with permission, and earn repeat business through reliable work.
---

Ari, 17, offers basic bike cleaning for neighbors. Posting "best service ever" everywhere would not explain what a customer gets. A useful offer says who it is for, what is included, the price, and how to book. Every price and result below is hypothetical.

**Marketing** helps people learn about an offer. Keeping customers depends on doing what you promised and handling problems fairly. Neither a flyer nor a happy customer guarantees another sale.

## Say what you can actually do

The [FTC's advertising guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business) says advertising must be truthful and supported by evidence. Ari should not promise repair skills or a safety inspection he cannot provide.

```table Ari rewrites a flyer
| Unclear or unsupported | Clearer offer |
| Makes every bike safe | Basic exterior cleaning; no repairs or safety inspection |
| Cheapest in town | $20 for the listed cleaning service |
| Available anytime | Saturday appointments from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. |
| Everyone loves it | Describe the work without inventing reviews |
```

Before posting, Ari asks permission to use a neighborhood board. He checks the board's rules and local requirements. He agrees on the work, full price, location, and cancellation terms before each job. A trusted adult can help him arrange safe meetings and check requirements for minors.

## Try one channel and count results

A **marketing channel** is a way to reach possible customers, such as a permitted flyer or a community announcement. Ari asks new customers how they heard about him, without collecting unnecessary personal details. He compares completed paid jobs with what the test cost.

```ledger Ari's hypothetical flyer test
Three completed jobs at $20 | $60.00
- Supplies used, three jobs at $3 | $9.00
- Printing cost for the test | $12.00
= Left before other costs, taxes, and paying himself | $39.00
~ People who took a flyer | 15
~ Customers who paid for completed jobs | 3
```

The $60 of sales is not $60 of profit. This small result also does not prove the flyer caused every sale or predict next month. Ari can ask for feedback and try a small change instead of spending heavily on more ads. The [SBA's marketing guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/marketing-sales) recommends tracking marketing costs and results.

## Ask permission before follow-up or sharing

A customer's phone number for arranging a job is not permission to send repeated promotions. Ask whether they want another message, respect a no, and follow applicable messaging rules. Do not copy a school roster or a group chat into a marketing list.

Ari also asks before posting a customer's bike photo, name, or review. He keeps identifying details such as a house number out of photos. If he offers a reward for a referral or review, that connection must be disclosed clearly; never require a positive review. See the [FTC's endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).

```table Ari handles customer information
| Situation | Better next step |
| Needs a way to confirm pickup | Ask for one contact method and explain why |
| Wants to post a before-and-after photo | Get permission and remove identifying details |
| Customer wants to recommend him | Let the customer pass along Ari's business contact |
| Has no ongoing need for a customer's details | Follow a secure deletion plan and required recordkeeping rules |
```

The [FTC's information-security guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business) recommends keeping only needed information and protecting it. Do not request payment-card details in a chat or keep passwords, door codes, or other unnecessary secrets in your customer notes.

## Make the next experience reliable

Confirm the appointment, arrive when promised, and tell the customer promptly if plans change. If a cleaning misses part of the agreed work, listen and offer a fair correction under the terms you gave. Do not hide a complaint or pressure someone into praise.

Before your next offer, write one clear description, choose a place where you have permission to share it, and decide how you will track completed jobs and respond to a problem.

```takeaways
- Describe the work, full price, and limits truthfully.
- Test a marketing channel with a spending limit and count paid results.
- Ask permission for promotions, photos, testimonials, and referrals involving personal details.
- Keep only needed customer information and deliver the work you agreed to do.
```
