Things go wrong: a package never arrives, a store charges you twice, a "free trial" keeps billing you. As an adult customer, you have real tools to fix these problems. Knowing them makes you calmer and more confident when you need them.

## Returns are up to the store

There is no federal law that says a store must take something back. Each store sets its own **return policy**, and some states require stores to post it. Before you buy something expensive, check the policy: how many days you have, whether you need a receipt, and whether you get cash back or only store credit. Keep receipts, or the order email, until you are sure you are keeping the item.

## Disputing a charge

If you were charged for something you did not buy, charged twice, or paid for something that never came, start with the seller. If that does not work, you can **dispute** the charge with your card company. When the card company reverses the charge, it is called a **chargeback**.

Jada, 18, checks her credit card statement and sees a $45 hoodie charged twice.

```ledger Jada's credit card statement
Groceries | $32.40
+ Hoodie | $45.00
+ Hoodie, charged a second time | $45.00
= Statement balance | $122.40
- Duplicate charge she disputes | $45.00
= What Jada actually owes | $77.40
```

The seller does not reply, so Jada disputes the charge with her card company. The online dispute form in her card's app works, but a written record is best.

```message Jada's dispute
Channel: email
From: Jada Brooks (you)
Subject: Billing error on account ending 4821
I am disputing a $45.00 charge from Threadline Apparel on September 14. I was charged twice for one order. I contacted the seller on September 16 and have not heard back. Please remove the duplicate charge. My order receipt is attached.
```

## Credit cards protect you more than debit cards

A federal law called the **Fair Credit Billing Act** covers billing errors on credit cards. You can dispute a charge in writing within 60 days after the statement with the error was sent to you. The card company has to look into it, and you do not have to pay the disputed amount while it does.

Debit cards are covered by a different law, and the rules are less forgiving. With a debit card, the money has already left your account while the bank investigates.

```table Credit card or debit card?
| If someone uses your card without permission | Credit card | Debit card |
| Most you can lose by law | $50 | $50 if you report within 2 business days of noticing the card is lost or stolen |
| If you wait | Still capped at $50 | Up to $500, or more if you wait past 60 days after your statement |
| Your money during a dispute | You do not pay the disputed amount | Already gone until the bank decides |
```

Many card companies promise $0 liability for fraud, but the legal limits are the floor. Whatever card you use, report problems fast.

## When a company will not help

If you tried to fix a problem and got nowhere:

- For a bank, credit card, loan, or payment app, file a complaint with the CFPB at [consumerfinance.gov/complaint](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/). The company has to respond.
- For a scam or unfair business practice, report it to the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).
- Your state attorney general's office also handles consumer complaints. You can find yours through [usa.gov](https://www.usa.gov).

```takeaways
- Return policies are set by each store, so check before you buy.
- You can dispute wrong or unauthorized charges; with credit cards, do it in writing within 60 days.
- Credit cards usually protect you better than debit cards.
- If a company will not fix a financial problem, file a complaint with the CFPB.
```
