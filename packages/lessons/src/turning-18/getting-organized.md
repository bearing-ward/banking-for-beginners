As an adult, you will be asked for documents at the worst moments: a new job wants your Social Security card, a college wants your tax return, a bank wants proof of address. Being organized is not about being perfect. It is about knowing where things are and catching problems early.

## Your important documents

Keep the originals of hard-to-replace documents in one safe place at home, like a folder in a drawer or a small fireproof box. For everyday use, a photo or scan saved in a secure, password-protected place is often enough.

```table What to keep and where
| Document | Keep the original | Why you need it |
| Birth certificate | Safe place at home | Passport, driver's license, some jobs |
| Social Security card | Safe place at home, not your wallet | New jobs, bank accounts, taxes |
| Passport or state ID | Safe place, or with you when needed | Proof of identity |
| W-2s and tax returns | Folder or secure scan, for at least 3 years | Loans, financial aid, fixing tax problems |
| Leases, loan papers, phone contracts | Folder or secure scan, until you are done with them | Proof of what you agreed to |
```

## Passwords and two-factor authentication

Your email and bank accounts are the keys to everything else. If someone gets into your email, they can reset most of your other passwords.

- Use a different, long password for each important account. A **password manager** can create and remember them for you.
- Turn on **two-factor authentication** (2FA) for email, banking, and payment apps. It asks for a second proof, like a code or an app prompt, when you log in.
- Never share a 2FA code with anyone. A real company will not ask for it.
- Keep your phone's lock screen on, and update your apps and phone software.

## A 15-minute monthly money check-in

Once a month, sit down with your banking app and check where things stand. Pick a day you will remember, like the day after your first paycheck of the month.

Here is what Dev, 18, sees in October from his part-time barista job.

```ledger Dev's checking account in October
Balance on October 1 | $640.00
+ Paychecks | $1,180.00
- Rent share and phone bill | $520.00
- Food and gas | $310.00
- Fun and subscriptions | $145.00
- Moved to savings | $200.00
= Balance on October 31 | $645.00
```

Dev's balance matches what the app shows, so nothing is missing. Then he runs through a short checklist:

1. Scan every transaction. Do you recognize each one?
2. Look for subscriptions you forgot about, and cancel the ones you do not use.
3. Check that bills were paid on time.
4. Move money to savings if you have not already.
5. Once a year or so, check your credit reports for free at [annualcreditreport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com).

> [!tip] Turn on account alerts in your bank app for low balances, large purchases, and deposits. Alerts catch fraud and mistakes faster than any monthly check.

The FTC has tips on protecting your accounts at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov), and the CFPB has budgeting tools at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov).

```takeaways
- Keep originals of hard-to-replace documents in one safe place, not your wallet.
- Use unique passwords, a password manager, and two-factor authentication.
- Spend 15 minutes a month checking transactions, subscriptions, and bills.
- Turn on alerts so problems reach you fast.
```
