Marcus, 18, works at a hardware store and wants a car to get to work and community college. He finds a used sedan listed for $11,500. That is the price everyone talks about, but it is not what the car will cost him.

The full cost of a car is called the **total cost of ownership**: the price, plus taxes and fees, interest on any loan, insurance, gas, and maintenance.

## The drive-off price

When you buy a car, the state charges sales tax, and you pay to title and register the car in your name. Dealers often add a documentation fee too. Tax rates and fees depend on your state.

```ledger What Marcus pays to drive it home
Price of the car | $11,500.00
+ Sales tax (6%) | $690.00
+ Title, registration, and dealer fees | $310.00
= Total cost | $12,500.00
- Down payment from savings | $2,500.00
= Amount Marcus borrows | $10,000.00
```

## The loan

Marcus gets a loan for $10,000 at 9% APR. The **APR** (annual percentage rate) is the yearly cost of borrowing. The longer the loan, the lower the monthly payment, but the more interest he pays in total.

```table $10,000 at 9% APR
| Loan length | Monthly payment | Total interest | Total paid |
| 36 months | $318.00 | $1,448.00 | $11,448.00 |
| 48 months | $248.85 | $1,944.80 | $11,944.80 |
| 60 months | $207.58 | $2,454.80 | $12,454.80 |
```

He picks 48 months. A shorter loan saves interest; a longer one can leave you owing more than the car is worth.

> [!tip] Get a loan offer from a bank or credit union before you visit a dealer. Then you can compare it with the dealer's financing and pick the lower APR.

## The monthly cost of owning it

The loan payment is not the whole monthly bill. Car insurance for a new driver under 25 is often the biggest surprise. Ask for quotes before you buy, since the model of car changes the price.

```ledger Marcus's car, every month
Loan payment | $248.85
+ Insurance | $180.00
+ Gas | $120.00
+ Maintenance and repair fund | $50.00
= Monthly cost of the car | $598.85
```

That is close to $600 a month for a car with an $11,500 price tag. If Marcus nets about $1,500 a month, the car takes about 40% of his pay. Seeing that before he signs helps him decide whether to look for a cheaper car, a bigger down payment, or a shorter commute.

## Before you buy a used car

- Get a vehicle history report and check for open safety recalls at [nhtsa.gov](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).
- Pay a mechanic you choose to inspect it before you buy.
- Negotiate the total price, not the monthly payment.
- Read the contract for add-ons you did not ask for, like extended warranties.

The FTC explains your rights when buying a car at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov), and the CFPB has a guide to auto loans at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov). Try the loan calculator to test your own numbers.

```takeaways
- The price is only the start. Add taxes, fees, loan interest, insurance, gas, and maintenance.
- Longer loans have lower payments but cost more in total interest.
- Get insurance quotes and a loan offer before you shop.
- Have a mechanic you choose inspect any used car before you pay.
```
