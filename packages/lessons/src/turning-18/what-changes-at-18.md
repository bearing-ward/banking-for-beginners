---
title: What changes at 18
level: starter
summary: On your 18th birthday you become a legal adult in most states. Contracts you sign count, your credit is your own, and some doors open while others close to your parents.
---

Turning 18 does not feel very different the morning after. Legally, though, a lot changes. In most states 18 is the **age of majority**, the age when the law treats you as an adult. (In Alabama and Nebraska it is 19, and in Mississippi it is 21, for most purposes.)

That means more freedom: you can open accounts, sign leases, and make your own decisions. It also means the responsibility for those decisions is yours.

```table Before and after 18
| | Before 18 | At 18 |
| Signing a contract | Usually can be undone | Binding: you have to keep it |
| Bank accounts | Often need a parent on the account | You can open one in your own name |
| Credit card | Only as an authorized user on a parent's card | Your own card, but under 21 you need your own income or a cosigner |
| School records | Parents can see them | Your records are yours; parents usually need your permission |
| Medical decisions | Parents usually decide | You decide; your doctor may not share details with your parents |
```

## Contracts become binding

A **contract** is an agreement the law will enforce: a phone plan, an apartment lease, a car loan, a gym membership. Before 18, most contracts you sign can be canceled because you are a minor. After 18, they cannot. If you sign up for something, you are on the hook for it, even if you change your mind.

That is why the next lesson is about reading a contract before you sign.

## Your credit is your own

Your **credit history** is the record of how you handle borrowed money. At 18, you can start building one in your own name. Lenders, landlords, and some employers will check it. Paying every bill on time is the most important habit you can start now.

## Parents lose automatic access

Your parents can no longer see some records only because they are your parents, including your college grades and your medical information. If you want them to be able to help, you can give permission. Colleges and doctors have forms for this.

> [!tip] Many families sign a simple form so a parent can talk to your doctor in an emergency. Ask your doctor's office about it before you leave for college or move out.

## Your 18th birthday checklist

```table Things to do around 18
| Task | Where to start |
| Register to vote | vote.gov (some states let you pre-register at 16 or 17) |
| Selective Service, if required | sss.gov |
| Check your credit reports for errors or fraud | annualcreditreport.com |
| Move accounts into your own name, or add yourself as owner | Your bank |
| Save copies of important documents | See the lesson on getting organized |
```

**Voting.** You can register at [vote.gov](https://vote.gov), which sends you to your state's official registration.

**Selective Service.** Almost all men ages 18 through 25 who live in the US are required by law to register with the Selective Service System, within 30 days of turning 18. It is a list, not a draft. Details are at [sss.gov](https://www.sss.gov).

Your credit reports are free at [annualcreditreport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com). The CFPB has more on building credit at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov).

```takeaways
- In most states, 18 is when contracts become binding and you become a legal adult.
- Your credit history starts to matter. Pay every bill on time.
- Parents lose automatic access to some school and medical records unless you give permission.
- Register to vote, and register with Selective Service if it applies to you.
```
