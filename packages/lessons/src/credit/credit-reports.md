Your credit score is a summary. The details live in your **credit report**: a record of your credit accounts and how you have paid them. Three companies, called **credit bureaus**, keep these reports: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Each keeps its own copy, so the three can differ.

Lenders, landlords, and some employers and insurers look at your report before they say yes. If it has a mistake, you could pay more or get turned down for something you deserved.

## What is in a report

```table What a credit report shows
| Section | Example |
| Who you are | Name, addresses, date of birth |
| Accounts | A student card opened in 2026, $500 limit |
| Payment record | Paid on time 14 of 14 months |
| Inquiries | A car lender checked your credit in March |
| Collections | A medical bill sent to a debt collector |
```

A report does not include your score. You can usually see a score for free through your bank or card company.

## Get your reports free

You can get a free report from each of the three bureaus every week at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com). It is the official site the law set up for this. You never need to pay or sign up for a trial to see your reports.

Copycat sites use names and ads that look similar and then try to sell you something. Type the address yourself rather than clicking an ad.

## Fix what is wrong

Read each report line by line. Look for accounts you never opened, late payments you know you made on time, or addresses where you never lived. If something is wrong, **dispute** it: tell the bureau that shows the error, in writing or online, and explain why, with copies of any proof. Also tell the company that reported it. The bureau generally has to investigate, usually within 30 days. The [CFPB explains the steps](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-an-error-on-my-credit-report-en-314/).

## A report you should not have

If you are under 18 and have never borrowed, you should not have a credit report at all. If one exists, someone may be using your information. That is **identity theft**. Report it at [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov) and ask a parent to help you contact the bureaus.

A **credit freeze** stops new lenders from seeing your report, which blocks most new accounts in your name. Freezes are free, and a parent can place one for a child under 16. You can lift it whenever you want to apply for credit.

```takeaways
- Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion each keep a credit report on you.
- Get all three free every week at AnnualCreditReport.com.
- Dispute errors with the bureau and the company that reported them.
- A report in a young teen's name can be a sign of identity theft.
```
