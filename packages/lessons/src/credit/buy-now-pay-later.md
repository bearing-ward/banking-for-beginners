summary: Splitting a purchase into four payments feels free, and sometimes it is. Here is how it works and how a few small plans can add up fast.

You are checking out online for $200 headphones, and a button offers: "4 payments of $50. 0% interest." That is **buy now, pay later**, or BNPL. You pay a quarter today and the rest in three more payments, usually every two weeks, taken automatically from your debit card or bank account.

BNPL is a loan. You are getting the headphones now and paying later. It can cost nothing if every payment goes through on time, which is why it is popular. The risk is in what happens when one does not.

```table Kiara's headphones, $200 in four payments
| Payment | When | Amount |
| 1 | At checkout | $50.00 |
| 2 | 2 weeks later | $50.00 |
| 3 | 4 weeks later | $50.00 |
| 4 | 6 weeks later | $50.00 |
```

## Where it goes wrong

- **Late fees.** If a payment fails because your account is short, you may owe a late fee, and your bank may charge an overdraft or returned-payment fee too.
- **Stacking.** Each plan feels small, so it is easy to have several running at once, all drawing from the same paycheck.
- **Longer plans charge interest.** Plans that stretch over months, often for bigger purchases, can have an APR as high as a credit card's.
- **Returns are messy.** If you return the item, you may need to keep paying until the refund is processed.
- **Credit reporting varies.** Some companies report your payments to the credit bureaus and some do not, so on-time payments may not help your score, but missed ones can still end up with a debt collector.

## How small plans stack up

Kiara has three plans going: the headphones, a $120 pair of shoes, and $100 concert tickets. In one week, all three take a payment from the same account her paycheck goes into.

```ledger Kiara's paycheck in a week when three plans come due
Paycheck | $277.05
- Headphones, payment 3 of 4 | $50.00
- Shoes, payment 2 of 4 | $30.00
- Concert tickets, payment 2 of 4 | $25.00
= Left for everything else | $172.05
```

More than a third of her check is gone before she buys gas or lunch. If she also has a phone bill due that week, a payment could fail.

## Using it safely

Before you click the button, ask yourself: could I pay for this today in full with money I have? If yes, BNPL is a convenience. If no, it is a loan you may not be able to repay. Keep a list of every plan and its payment dates, and avoid having more than one going at a time.

The [CFPB has a short guide to BNPL](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-loan-en-2119/).

```takeaways
- Buy now, pay later is a loan, even when it charges no interest.
- Missed payments can bring late fees and bank overdraft fees.
- Several small plans can take a big bite out of one paycheck.
- Use it only for things you could afford to pay for today.
```
