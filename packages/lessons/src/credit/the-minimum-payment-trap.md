The **minimum payment** on a credit card is the smallest amount you can pay and avoid a late fee. It looks friendly: a $1,000 balance might ask for only about $30. But the minimum is set so the card company keeps earning interest. Paying only that much is the slowest, most expensive way out of debt.

Dev put $1,000 of car repairs and holiday gifts on a card with a 24% APR. Look at where his first $30 payment goes.

```ledger Dev's first month at $30
~ APR | 24%, or 2% a month
Balance | $1,000.00
+ Interest this month | $20.00
- Dev's payment | $30.00
= New balance | $990.00
```

Of his $30, $20 went to interest. Only $10 made the balance smaller.

## How long it really takes

Say Dev stops using the card and pays the same amount every month until it is gone. Here is what different payments cost, figured the same way as the calculator beside this lesson.

```table Paying off $1,000 at 24% APR
| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Interest paid | Total paid |
| $30 | 4 years, 8 months | $664.43 | $1,664.43 |
| $50 | 2 years, 2 months | $289.88 | $1,289.88 |
| $100 | 1 year | $127.04 | $1,127.04 |
```

Paying $100 instead of $30 saves Dev $537.39 and more than three and a half years of payments.

Real minimums are often worse than this table. Many card companies figure the minimum as a small share of your balance plus interest, so the minimum shrinks as you pay down, and the payoff drags on even longer. Your statement has a box that shows how long you will be paying if you only make the minimum. Read it.

## When the payment never wins

If your payment is no bigger than the interest, the balance never goes down. At 24% APR, $1,000 charges $20 of interest in a month. Pay $20 a month and you will still owe $1,000 forever.

```ledger Paying only the interest
Balance | $1,000.00
+ Interest this month | $20.00
- Payment | $20.00
= Balance next month | $1,000.00
```

## Getting out

- Stop adding new charges to the card you are paying off.
- Pay a fixed amount well above the minimum, every month, on autopay.
- If you have more than one card, put extra money toward the one with the highest APR first.
- If you are stuck, call the card company and ask about a lower rate or a payment plan. A nonprofit credit counselor can help too; the [CFPB explains how to find one](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/).

> [!tip] Put your own balance, APR, and payment into the calculator beside this lesson. Then add $20 to the payment and see how much time and interest it saves.

```takeaways
- The minimum payment keeps you on time, not out of debt.
- Early on, most of a small payment goes to interest.
- A payment at or below the monthly interest never pays the balance off.
- Pay a fixed amount well above the minimum, and stop adding charges.
```
