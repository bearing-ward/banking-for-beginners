---
title: Challenge your plan for a surprise bill
level: next-step
summary: Check a repair bill, protect essential costs, and rebuild a plan after using emergency savings.
---

An emergency fund is there to be used when something important goes wrong. This practice story combines checking a bill, protecting essentials, and deciding what can wait. The amounts are fictional.

## Try the decision first

Jordan, 18, needs a bicycle to get to work. A repair shop quotes $180 for a necessary repair. Jordan has $120 in checking, a $40 phone bill due tomorrow, and needs $50 for food and transport before payday. There is also $200 in emergency savings. A $60 concert ticket is optional and has not been bought.

How much checking money is available for the repair after reserving essentials? How much must come from emergency savings? Should the concert ticket stay in this week's plan?

```ledger Checking money available for the repair
Checking balance | $120.00
- Phone bill reserve | $40.00
- Food and transport reserve | $50.00
= Available toward repair | $30.00
```

## Confirm the cost, then make the plan

Before approving work, Jordan asks for an itemized quote and checks whether the repair is needed now. The confirmed price is $180 with no extra fees. Other repair options would leave Jordan without transport for upcoming shifts.

```ledger Paying the confirmed repair bill
Repair cost | $180.00
- Checking contribution | $30.00
= Needed from emergency savings | $150.00
```

Jordan uses $150 from savings and postpones the concert ticket. This protects essential bills and the ability to earn. It also leaves only $50 in the emergency fund, so another surprise would be harder to cover. The [CFPB guide to prioritizing bills](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_your-money-your-goals_prioritizing-bills_tool.pdf) suggests weighing the consequences of missing each payment.

## Recover after the surprise

At the next payday, Jordan checks actual bills before promising a savings transfer. If $30 is available each payday, five transfers would restore the $150 used. That is a plan, not a reason to skip food or a required payment.

```ledger Rebuilding the emergency fund
Savings after repair | $50.00
+ Five future transfers of $30 | $150.00
= Restored emergency fund | $200.00
```

If the confirmed repair cost were greater than Jordan could cover, the next step would be to ask about safe alternatives or written payment terms, and seek local assistance. Do not assume a creditor must offer a plan. Compare the full repayment cost before borrowing, and do not promise payments the budget cannot support.

```takeaways
- Reserve essentials before deciding what money is available.
- Confirm a bill and consider the consequences of delaying it.
- Emergency savings can protect your ability to work; rebuild them when the budget allows.
```
