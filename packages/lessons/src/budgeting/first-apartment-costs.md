Moving out is a big step, and the rent on the listing is only part of the price. Before you sign a **lease**, the contract that says how long you will rent and for how much, add up three things: what it costs to move in, what it costs each month, and how much you need to earn to keep it up.

## Move-in costs come all at once

Camila, 19, works full time as a dental office assistant. She found a one-bedroom for $1,100 a month. Here is what she needed before she got the keys.

```ledger Camila's move-in costs
First month's rent | $1,100.00
+ Security deposit | $1,100.00
+ Application fee | $50.00
+ Utility setup and deposits | $100.00
+ Moving truck and supplies | $150.00
+ Bed, kitchen basics, and cleaning supplies | $400.00
= Cash needed to move in | $2,900.00
```

A **security deposit** is money the landlord holds in case you damage the place or leave owing rent. You should get it back, minus any proper deductions, after you move out. Take dated photos of every room on move-in day so you can show what was already there.

> [!note] Deposit limits, the deadline for returning a deposit, and caps on application fees depend on your state, and sometimes your city. Search for your state attorney general's or housing agency's renter guide before you sign.

## Monthly costs beyond rent

```ledger Camila's monthly costs of living alone
Rent | $1,100.00
+ Electric | $70.00
+ Internet | $55.00
+ Renter's insurance | $15.00
+ Groceries | $300.00
+ Bus pass and rides | $120.00
+ Phone | $40.00
= Monthly cost of the basics | $1,700.00
```

**Renter's insurance** covers your own belongings if they are stolen or damaged, and pays if you accidentally damage someone else's property. The landlord's insurance covers the building, not your stuff. Some leases require it.

## The 30% rule of thumb

A common guideline is to keep rent at or below about **30% of your income before taxes**. To find the income a rent needs, divide the rent by 0.30.

```table Income needed for a rent, at 30%
| | Living alone | With a roommate |
| Monthly rent (your share) | $1,100.00 | $800.00 |
| Monthly income needed | $3,666.67 | $2,666.67 |
| Yearly income needed | $44,000 | $32,000 |
| Hourly wage at 40 hours a week, 52 weeks | $21.15 | $15.38 |
```

Camila earns $21.00 an hour, a little under what living alone calls for. She can make it work with a tight budget, or she can look for a roommate and drop her share to $800. Many landlords also check that your income is two to three times the rent before they approve you.

> [!tip] Start saving for move-in costs months ahead. A move-in fund is a perfect goal for paying yourself first.

```takeaways
- Moving in can cost two or more months of rent up front, before furniture.
- Budget for utilities, internet, renter's insurance, food, and transportation on top of rent.
- A common guideline: rent at or below about 30% of income before taxes.
- Deposit and fee rules depend on your state; read your lease and photograph the place on move-in day.
```
