---
title: Tracking your spending
level: starter
summary: A budget is the plan; tracking is how you find out what really happened. A few minutes a week is enough.
---

You made a budget. Two weeks later, your account is lower than you expected and you are not sure why. That gap is what **tracking** fixes. Tracking means writing down, or checking, what you actually spent and comparing it to your plan.

Most people underestimate small, frequent purchases: a drink here, a delivery fee there. Tracking is how they show up.

## Look at one category for a week

Nia, 17, works as a host at a restaurant. She budgets $200 a month for eating out. Here is her first week, from her bank app.

```ledger Nia's eating out, week 1
Monday: coffee before school | $5.75
Tuesday: lunch with friends | $11.40
Wednesday: food delivery | $23.85
Friday: boba | $6.50
Saturday: pizza after a shift | $14.20
= Week 1 total | $61.70
```

None of these felt big. Together they used almost a third of her monthly eating-out budget in one week.

```ledger Nia's eating-out budget for the month
Budgeted | $200.00
- Week 1 | $61.70
- Week 2 | $72.45
- Week 3 | $54.10
= Left for the last week | $11.75
```

Nia now has a choice: pack lunch for the last week, or move money from another category, like clothes, to cover it. Either is fine. Tracking let her decide on purpose instead of overdrawing her account.

## Pick a method you will keep using

```table Ways to track
| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
| Your bank's app | Seeing card purchases sorted by category | Cash and payment-app spending may not show clearly |
| A budgeting app | Automatic categories and alerts | Some charge a fee or ask to link your accounts |
| A spreadsheet | Full control, free | You have to enter things yourself |
| A notebook | Simple, no screen | Easy to forget a day |
```

The best method is the one you will still use in a month. If you share an app or spreadsheet with a parent, check that you are comfortable with what they can see.

## Make it a weekly habit

- Pick a day, like Sunday night, and spend five minutes.
- Check each category against your plan.
- Look for anything you do not recognize. A charge you did not make could be fraud, so report it to your bank right away.
- Change next month's budget if a number keeps being wrong.

> [!tip] Payment apps such as Venmo, Zelle, or Cash App are easy to forget. Add a note to each payment ("pizza, Sam's share") so you can track it later.

```takeaways
- A budget is the plan; tracking shows what really happened.
- Small, frequent purchases add up fastest.
- Check your spending once a week, category by category.
- If a category keeps running over, change the plan or the habit.
```
