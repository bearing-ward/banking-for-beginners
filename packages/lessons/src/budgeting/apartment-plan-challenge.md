A low advertised rent can still leave you without enough money to move. This practice story combines move-in costs, monthly budgeting, and checking a lease. All amounts are fictional, not estimates of rents in your area.

## Try the decision first

Eli, 18, has $1,800 saved and takes home $1,600 each month. A room costs $650 a month. Moving in requires the first month's rent, a $650 deposit, and $200 for moving and basic supplies. Eli wants to keep $500 for emergencies after moving.

Can Eli pay the move-in costs and keep that cushion? What information should Eli check before signing?

```ledger Cash needed before Eli moves
First month's rent | $650.00
+ Security deposit | $650.00
+ Moving and basic supplies | $200.00
= Move-in costs | $1,500.00
+ Desired emergency cushion | $500.00
= Savings target before moving | $2,000.00
```

The target is $200 above Eli's savings. The deposit is not spending money while the landlord holds it, even if Eli expects it back later. Eli could wait and save, seek a cheaper arrangement, or confirm whether any actual costs can be reduced. Do not count hoped-for help as cash already available.

## Check a normal month too

Eli lists the expected ongoing costs below. The first month's rent in the move-in ledger pays that month's rent; it is not an extra second rent charge. This monthly ledger describes a later normal month.

```ledger Eli's monthly plan
Take-home pay | $1,600.00
- Rent | $650.00
- Utilities and internet | $100.00
- Groceries | $250.00
- Transportation | $100.00
- Phone | $40.00
- Renters insurance | $15.00
- Household and personal costs | $100.00
- Emergency savings | $100.00
= Left for goals and flexible spending | $245.00
```

There is room in this plan, but it depends on the estimates being accurate. Ask which utilities are included, how they are split, and what fees the lease requires. Test the plan with actual bills and work hours. A smaller paycheck could use up the $245 quickly.

## Check the risk before the signature

Eli's next action is to close the $200 savings gap and confirm the lease terms. A monthly plan that works does not fix a move-in cash shortage.

```table Questions before signing
| Check | Why it matters |
| All tenants' responsibility for rent | A roommate leaving may increase what you owe |
| Deposit and move-out terms | Your state sets rules; a deposit is not automatically the last month's rent |
| Utilities and fees | The listing may omit costs you must pay |
| Move-in condition | Dated photos help document existing damage |
```

Use your [state's tenant-rights resources through USA.gov](https://www.usa.gov/tenant-rights) to check local rules. For unclear or disputed lease terms, ask a local legal aid organization before signing.

```takeaways
- Test both move-in cash and a normal monthly budget.
- Keep the deposit separate from money available for bills.
- Check lease responsibilities and local rules before signing.
```
