A **budget** is a plan that tells each dollar where to go before the month starts. It is not a punishment or a list of things you cannot have. It is how you make sure the bills get paid, the goals get funded, and the fun money is guilt-free.

## Start with your take-home pay

Build your budget on **net pay**, the amount that actually lands in your account, not your hourly wage. If your hours change, use a low but realistic month so the plan still works in a slow month.

Ava, 16, works at a smoothie shop and lives with her parents. She takes home about $520 a month.

```ledger Ava's monthly budget
Take-home pay | $520.00
- Savings for a car | $150.00
- Her share of the phone bill | $30.00
- Gas for the family car | $60.00
- Food out with friends | $100.00
- Fun: movies, games, events | $120.00
- Clothes | $60.00
= Left without a plan | $0.00
```

Every dollar has a job, so the bottom line is zero. That does not mean she spends it all: $150 of her "spending" is savings.

## A starting split: 50/30/20

When you pay for more of your own life, a common starting point is the **50/30/20** split: about half of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for saving and paying off debt. It is a rule of thumb, not a law. Use the calculator beside this lesson to see it for your pay.

Marcus, 18, finished high school, works full time at a warehouse, and shares an apartment. He takes home $2,400 a month.

```ledger Marcus's monthly budget
Take-home pay | $2,400.00
- Rent, his share | $750.00
- Utilities and internet, his share | $80.00
- Groceries | $260.00
- Bus pass | $60.00
- Phone | $40.00
- Eating out and coffee | $180.00
- Going out with friends | $250.00
- Streaming and gym | $48.00
- Clothes | $100.00
- Spending money for anything | $132.00
- Emergency fund | $300.00
- Car fund | $200.00
= Left without a plan | $0.00
```

```table How Marcus compares to 50/30/20
| | 50/30/20 target | Marcus's plan |
| Needs | $1,200 | $1,190 |
| Wants | $720 | $710 |
| Saving | $480 | $500 |
```

His needs are the first five lines, his wants are the next five, and the last two are saving. He is close to the split. If rent were higher, he would trim wants first.

## Build yours in four steps

1. Write down your monthly take-home pay.
2. List your needs and what they cost. Use real bills or bank history, not guesses.
3. Decide on savings, and put it near the top so it gets paid first.
4. Split what is left among your wants until the bottom line is zero.

> [!tip] A budget is a draft. Check it after the first month and change the numbers that were wrong. Most people's first budget is off in a few places.

```takeaways
- A budget plans every dollar before the month starts.
- Build it on take-home pay, using a realistic low month.
- 50/30/20 (needs, wants, saving) is a starting point, not a rule.
- Put saving near the top, and revise the budget after the first month.
```
