For a lot of teens, the phone is the first bill that is partly or fully theirs. It is also one of the easiest to overpay, because the cost is split into pieces: the plan, a payment for the phone itself, protection, and taxes and fees.

## Add up the real monthly cost

Tyler, 18, works at a gym front desk and recently got his own line with a big carrier. He picked the top unlimited plan and a new phone with no money down.

```ledger Tyler's real monthly phone cost
Unlimited plan | $70.00
+ Phone payment, $720 over 24 months | $30.00
+ Device protection | $15.00
+ Taxes and fees | $8.40
= Total each month | $123.40
```

"No money down" did not mean free. The $720 phone is paid in 24 pieces, and if Tyler leaves the carrier early, he usually has to pay off the rest of the phone at once.

> [!note] Taxes and fees on phone bills depend on your state and city, so the same plan can cost a few dollars more or less depending on where you live.

## Compare it to a cheaper setup

Tyler checked his settings and saw he uses about 8 GB of data a month, mostly on Wi-Fi. A **prepaid plan**, which you pay for at the start of each month with no contract, with 15 GB would cover him. He could buy a used phone up front.

```table Two ways to have a phone
| | Tyler's setup | Cheaper setup |
| Plan | Unlimited, $70.00 | Prepaid 15 GB, $25.00 |
| Phone | New, $720 over 24 months | Used, $240 paid once |
| Protection | $15.00 a month | A sturdy case |
| Taxes and fees | $8.40 | $3.00 |
| Monthly total | $123.40 | $28.00 |
```

```ledger Two-year cost, Tyler's setup minus the cheaper one
Tyler's setup, $123.40 × 24 months | $2,961.60
- Cheaper setup, $28.00 × 24 months | $672.00
- Used phone, paid once | $240.00
= Difference over two years | $2,049.60
```

More than $2,000 over two years, for a phone that does mostly the same things. That is not a reason Tyler has to switch. It is the number he should weigh.

## Ways to pay less

- **Check your data use** in your phone settings before choosing a plan.
- **Stay on a family plan** if your family will keep you on it, and agree on what you pay toward it.
- **Look at prepaid plans**, which often run on the same networks as the big carriers.
- **Keep your phone longer.** A phone that is paid off cuts your bill.
- **Skip device protection** if a case and a small savings fund would cover a repair.

> [!tip] Before you switch carriers, make sure your phone is paid off and unlocked, and that you know how to keep your phone number.

```takeaways
- Your phone's real cost is the plan plus the phone payment, protection, and taxes and fees.
- "No money down" spreads the phone's price over your bill.
- Check your data use; many people pay for far more than they need.
- Prepaid plans and keeping your phone longer can save a lot over two years.
```
