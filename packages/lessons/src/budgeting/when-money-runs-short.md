---
title: When money runs short
level: starter
summary: Fewer shifts can leave you short before bills are due. Track the cash week by week, protect essentials, and ask early about hardship options and local help.
---

Your work schedule can change without warning. A **cash-flow budget** tracks when money comes in and bills go out, week by week. It can show a shortfall before a payment is due, while there is still time to ask for help. A shortfall is not a personal failure, and cutting optional spending cannot always cover it.

## Find the week that goes negative

Jordan, 18, works at a grocery store and usually gets three shifts a week. This month, they got fewer shifts and have $85 in checking before payday. A $200 electric bill is due in Week 2. The amounts below are fictional, and all transactions occur in the order shown.

```ledger Jordan's original plan reaches a shortfall
Week 1 beginning balance | $85.00
+ Week 1 paycheck | $120.00
- Bus fare | $20.00
- Groceries | $35.00
= Week 1 ending balance | $150.00
+ Week 2 paycheck | $90.00
- Bus fare | $20.00
- Phone bill | $45.00
- Electric bill due | $200.00
= Week 2 projected shortfall | -$25.00
```

The negative $25 is a warning, not money Jordan can spend. The bill cannot be paid in full from this account under the original schedule. Even if the month ends positive, the timing can still cause a missed payment. The [CFPB cash-flow budget](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/your-money-your-goals/toolkit/) uses this week-to-week approach.

## Protect essentials and contact people early

List what must be covered to stay safe, healthy, housed, and able to get to school or work: food, medication, housing, utilities, and transportation. Pause optional purchases and subscriptions while you figure out the gap. Do not skip food, medication, or a safe place to stay to make a spreadsheet balance.

If you still cannot cover a bill, contact the company or person you owe before the due date. Ask what hardship options are available, such as a changed due date, payment plan, or temporary assistance. Write down who you spoke with, when, and what they said. A plan may not be available, so ask what happens if you can pay only part. Jordan can ask a trusted adult to join the call.

```message Jordan asks the electric company for options
Channel: call
From: Jordan
I had fewer work shifts this month, and I expect to be short on my electric bill. I can pay $100 by the due date. Are there hardship plans, a different due date, or local assistance programs I can ask about? Could you explain any fees or deadlines and send the details in writing?
```

If a household needs food, rent, utility, or other basic-needs help, [United Way 211](https://211.org/get-help/i-need-help-paying-my-bills) can connect people with local resources. You can call 211 or search for your area's 211 service; available programs and eligibility depend on location. A school counselor, social worker, community center, or trusted adult can help you make the call. Assistance may take time and may not cover the whole bill, so keep talking with the company while you apply.

## Revise only after an agreement

In this story, the company confirms in writing that Jordan can pay $100 in Week 2 and $100 in Week 4, with no added fee or service interruption while following the agreement. This is a fictional arrangement, not a promise that a company must offer one. Jordan checks the terms and updates the plan.

```ledger Jordan's revised four-week plan
Week 1 ending balance | $150.00
+ Week 2 paycheck | $90.00
- Week 2 bus fare | $20.00
- Week 2 phone bill | $45.00
- First agreed electric payment | $100.00
= Week 2 ending balance | $75.00
+ Week 3 paycheck | $90.00
- Week 3 bus fare | $20.00
- Week 3 groceries | $35.00
= Week 3 ending balance | $110.00
+ Week 4 paycheck | $90.00
- Week 4 bus fare | $20.00
- Week 4 groceries | $35.00
- Final agreed electric payment | $100.00
= Week 4 ending balance | $45.00
```

The revised balances stay positive, and the whole electric bill is covered. Jordan still has only a small cushion. If pay drops again or another cost appears, update the plan and contact the company again. Do not treat an unanswered request as an agreement to delay payment.

```takeaways
- Carry each week's ending balance into the next week's starting balance.
- Protect essentials first, and pause optional spending while you make a plan.
- Contact the company before the due date and ask about hardship options.
- 211 and trusted local adults can help find assistance; eligibility and availability vary.
```
