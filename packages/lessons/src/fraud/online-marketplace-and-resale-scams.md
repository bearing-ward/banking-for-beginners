---
title: Marketplace and resale scams
level: starter
summary: Sneakers, concert tickets, and gaming items are top targets for fake sellers and fake buyers. Pay with protection and meet in safe places.
---

Buying and selling with strangers online can get you a great deal on sneakers, a sold-out concert ticket, or a rare skin for your favorite game. It is also where many teens first meet a scammer. The scam is usually simple: you pay, and nothing arrives.

The two things that protect you most are **how you pay** and **where you meet**.

## When you are buying

Fake sellers post items at a price that is a little too good, then push you to pay in a way you cannot undo.

```message A sneaker deal in your DMs
Channel: dm
From: @kicks.plug.resell
Yeah they're still available, $140 shipped (retail is $220 lol). Only 1 pair left and 3 people asking. Send it friends and family on the app so there's no fees, I'll ship today
! Price well under what they sell for
! Pressure from "other buyers"
! Wants a payment with no buyer protection
```

Ticket scams work the same way: a screenshot of a ticket, a seller who "can't transfer it yet," and a request to pay on a payment app. Screenshots can be copied and sold to many people.

```table Ways to pay a stranger
| Payment | Protection if it never shows up |
| Credit card | Strong: you can dispute the charge |
| Marketplace's own checkout | Often covered by the site's buyer guarantee |
| Debit card | Some, but weaker than a credit card |
| Payment app, friends and family | Usually none |
| Gift cards, crypto, wire | None |
```

> [!tip] For tickets, buy from the official ticket seller or a resale site that guarantees your ticket. For gaming items, trade only inside the game's official trade system. Trades "off-platform" are where accounts and items get stolen.

## When you are selling

Scammers pose as buyers too. Watch for:

- A buyer who sends more than your price and asks for the difference back. (See the lesson on payment app scams.)
- A buyer who wants to send a verification code to your phone "to make sure you're real." That code lets them take over your account.
- A buyer who pays with a check, or asks you to ship before payment arrives.

```message A "buyer" who wants a code
Channel: text
From: +1 (248) 555-0191
Hi, is the bike still for sale? I've been scammed before so I need to verify you're real. I'm sending a 6-digit code to your phone, can you read it back to me?
! Asks for a code sent to your phone
! A sympathy story to lower your guard
```

## Meeting in person

For local sales, meet in a busy public place during the day. Many police departments have a marked **safe exchange zone** in their parking lot or lobby. Bring a friend or a parent, and tell someone where you are going. Do not go to a stranger's home or invite them to yours.

Check the item before you pay. For a phone or console, turn it on and make sure it is not locked to another account.

If a deal goes wrong, report the seller or buyer to the site or app, and report it at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). The FTC explains how to buy safely at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov).

```takeaways
- Pay strangers with a credit card or a marketplace's checkout, never gift cards, crypto, or "friends and family."
- A price far below what the item usually sells for, with pressure to pay now, is a red flag.
- Never read a verification code to a buyer.
- Meet in a busy public place, like a police safe exchange zone, and bring someone.
```

```quiz
? A seller wants you to pay for concert tickets with a payment app's "friends and family" option. What is the risk?
+ If the tickets are fake, you probably cannot get your money back
- You will pay extra fees
- The tickets will arrive more slowly
> "Friends and family" payments usually have no buyer protection. Scammers ask for them for that reason.

? Which is the safest place to meet a buyer for your old phone?
- The buyer's apartment
- A quiet parking lot after dark
+ A police station's safe exchange zone during the day
> Busy, well-lit places with cameras, like a police safe exchange zone, keep both people honest and safe.

? A buyer asks you to read back a code they texted you. What should you do?
- Read it so they know you are a real seller
+ Refuse, because the code lets them into one of your accounts
- Read only the first three digits
> Verification codes protect your accounts. A buyer has no reason to need one.
```
