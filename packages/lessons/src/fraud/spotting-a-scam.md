title: Spotting a scam
level: starter
summary: Almost every scam uses the same few tricks. Learn the pattern once and you can spot it in a text, a DM, an email, or a phone call.
---

Scammers do not need to hack anything. They need you to act before you think. That is why scams look different on the surface (a prize, a job, a problem with your account, a friend in trouble) but follow the same script underneath.

The good news: once you know the script, you start seeing it everywhere. Being careful is not about being paranoid. It is about slowing down long enough to notice.

## The four tricks

Nearly every scam leans on at least one of these:

- **Urgency.** "Act in the next 30 minutes or lose your account." A deadline keeps you from checking.
- **Secrecy.** "Don't tell your parents" or "keep this between us." A scammer does not want a second opinion.
- **An unusual way to pay.** Gift cards, crypto, a wire transfer, or a payment app such as Venmo, Zelle, or Cash App. These are hard or impossible to reverse.
- **Too good to be true.** Free money, a prize you never entered, a job that pays $40 an hour for "easy tasks."

```message A giveaway DM
Channel: dm
From: @glowup.giveaways.official
Congrats!! You were picked as our $1,000 winner!! To release your prize, send a $25 processing fee with a gift card in the next hour. Don't post about it yet or you lose your spot!
! A prize for a contest you never entered
! You have to pay to get "free" money
! Gift card payment and a one-hour deadline
! Asks you to keep it quiet
```

## Why the payment method matters

Real businesses and government agencies do not ask you to pay with gift cards, crypto, or a wire transfer. They also do not ask you to send money on a payment app to "verify" anything. Scammers like these methods because the money moves fast and is very hard to get back.

```table How a scam asks to be paid
| Payment | Why scammers like it |
| Gift cards | Once you share the numbers on the back, the money is gone |
| Crypto | Hard to trace and almost never reversible |
| Wire transfer | Moves fast; banks can rarely pull it back |
| Payment app | Works like handing over cash |
```

## Your pause button

When a message makes you feel rushed, scared, or excited, treat that feeling as the signal to stop. Then:

1. Do not click links or call numbers in the message.
2. Look up the company, school, or person yourself and contact them through a number or app you already trust.
3. Tell someone: a parent, a friend, a teacher. Saying it out loud often makes the trick obvious.

Real problems can wait 10 minutes while you check. Only scams cannot.

> [!tip] Smart people get scammed every day, including adults who handle money for a living. Falling for a trick says something about the scammer's skill, not about you.

The FTC keeps a list of the latest scams at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov). If you spot one, you can report it at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov), even if you did not lose money.

```takeaways
- Most scams use urgency, secrecy, an unusual payment method, or an offer that is too good to be true.
- Gift cards, crypto, wires, and payment apps are a scammer's favorite ways to get paid.
- A rushed or excited feeling is your cue to pause and check on your own.
- Contact the real company or person through a number or app you already trust.
```

```quiz
? A text says you won a $500 gift card and must pay $10 shipping within an hour. What is the biggest red flag?
- It came by text instead of email
+ You have to pay, fast, to get a free prize
- The prize is a gift card
> A real prize does not cost money to claim, and the one-hour deadline is there to stop you from checking.

? Which payment request should make you most suspicious?
- A store asking you to tap your debit card at the register
- Your school asking for a field trip fee through its usual parent portal
+ Someone asking you to pay with gift cards and read them the numbers
> Gift cards are for gifts. When someone wants the card numbers to "pay" a bill or fee, it is a scam.

? A message tells you not to tell your parents about an offer. What does that usually mean?
+ The sender does not want anyone to check the story
- The offer is exclusive and real
- You are old enough to decide alone, so it is fine
> Secrecy is a classic scam trick. Talking it over with someone is one of the best ways to see through it.
```
