**Identity theft** is when someone uses your personal information, like your name, birthday, and Social Security number (SSN), to open accounts, borrow money, or get a job in your name. Teens are a common target because nobody is checking their credit yet. A thief can use a teen's identity for years before anyone notices, often when the teen applies for a first car loan or apartment.

## Guard your Social Security number

Your SSN does not change, so a stolen one stays useful to a thief. Only give it out when there is a real reason, like a job, a bank account, a tax form, or financial aid.

```table Who really needs your SSN?
| Who is asking | Give your SSN? |
| Your new employer, for your W-4 and payroll | Yes |
| Your bank, when you open an account | Yes |
| A store that wants it for a loyalty card | No |
| A caller who says your SSN is "suspended" | No, hang up |
| A form at the doctor's office | Ask if it is required; often it is not |
```

Keep your card at home in a safe place, not in your wallet. Shred papers that show your SSN or account numbers. And never post a photo of your driver's license, a boarding pass, or a paycheck online.

## Freezing your credit

A **credit freeze** (also called a security freeze) locks your credit report so lenders cannot see it. Since most lenders check your report before opening a new account, a freeze stops a thief from opening credit in your name. It does not hurt your credit score.

- A freeze is **free** at all three national credit bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. You have to set it up at each one.
- When you need credit, like a car loan or a phone plan, you can lift the freeze for a day or two, then turn it back on.
- Parents can request a freeze for a child under 16, even before the child has a credit report.

```table Ways to protect your credit
| Tool | What it does | Cost |
| Credit freeze | Blocks new lenders from seeing your report | Free |
| Fraud alert | Tells lenders to verify it is you before opening credit; lasts one year | Free |
| Checking your credit report | Shows any accounts opened in your name | Free at annualcreditreport.com |
```

## Check for signs

Once you turn 18, check your credit reports at [annualcreditreport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com), the official free site. Look for accounts you did not open. Other warning signs at any age: bills or collection calls for things you never bought, or a tax return rejected because someone already filed with your SSN.

If someone has used your identity, go to [identitytheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov). It is the FTC's free site, and it builds a step-by-step recovery plan and the reports you need. The FTC explains credit freezes at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov).

```takeaways
- Share your SSN only when there is a real need, and keep your card at home.
- A credit freeze is free at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, and stops most new-account fraud.
- Parents can freeze credit for a child under 16.
- If your identity is stolen, start a recovery plan at identitytheft.gov.
```
