summary: Scammers posing as other teens trick people into sharing private images, then demand money. If it happens, it is not your fault, you are not alone, and there is a way out.
---

This lesson covers a hard topic, so here is the most important part first: **if this happens to you, it is not your fault, you are not in trouble, and help is available.** Thousands of teens have been targeted, and many have gotten through it with help.

**Sextortion** is when someone gets a private or explicit image of you and threatens to share it unless you pay them or send more. Many of these scammers are adults overseas running the same script on many teens at once. They are after money.

## How it usually happens

It often starts with a friendly message from someone who seems to be a teen around your age, with photos and a profile that look real. They flirt, move quickly, and may send an image first, which is usually stolen from someone else. Once you send something back, the tone changes in seconds.

```message The moment the scam shows itself
Channel: dm
From: @kayla.k_2009
I have your pic and I screenshotted all your followers. Send $300 in gift cards in the next 30 minutes or I send it to everyone, your school, your family. Don't think I'm joking.
! A fake profile that turns into a threat
! A tight deadline meant to cause panic
! Demands gift cards or a payment app
! Threatens to contact your friends and family
```

The panic you feel in that moment is exactly what the scammer wants. Slow down. You have more options than they want you to believe.

## What to do

```table If someone is threatening you
| Do | Don't |
| Stop replying, then block them | Pay, even a small amount |
| Save screenshots of the profile, messages, and usernames | Delete your account or the messages before saving them |
| Tell a trusted adult: a parent, relative, coach, or counselor | Send more images or try to negotiate |
| Report it to the platform and to NCMEC or the FBI | Handle it alone |
```

**Do not pay.** Paying almost never ends it. Scammers who get money usually ask for more.

**Tell a trusted adult.** This is the hardest step and the one that helps most. The adults who care about you would much rather know and help than have you carry this alone.

**Report it.** You can report to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children's CyberTipline at [report.cybertip.org](https://report.cybertip.org) or to the FBI at [tips.fbi.gov](https://tips.fbi.gov). Reporting can help stop the scammer from targeting others.

**Get the image taken down.** NCMEC's free **Take It Down** service at [takeitdown.ncmec.org](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org) helps remove or block private images taken when you were under 18 from participating platforms. It works without you sending the image to anyone.

> [!note] If you feel overwhelmed, hopeless, or unsafe, you can call or text 988 any time, day or night, to talk with someone who will listen.

## Lowering the risk

- Be careful with new online friends you have never met in person, especially ones who move fast or turn the conversation private.
- Keep your accounts private and limit who can see your followers and friends list.
- Remember that once an image is sent, you cannot control where it goes.

NCMEC has more support for teens and families at [missingkids.org](https://www.missingkids.org).

```takeaways
- Sextortion scammers pose as teens, get a private image, and demand money.
- It is not your fault. Do not pay, stop replying, and save the evidence.
- Tell a trusted adult and report it to the CyberTipline or the FBI.
- Take It Down can help remove images taken when you were under 18.
```
