---
title: Job and gig scams
level: starter
summary: Fake job offers target people looking for their first job. A real job pays you; a scam job asks you to pay, or to move packages and money for it.
---

When you are looking for your first job or a side gig, an offer that pays well and lets you work from your phone sounds great. Scammers know this. Fake job offers are one of the most common ways teens and young adults lose money.

The simplest test: **a real job pays you. It does not ask you to pay first.** Not for training, not for a starter kit, not for equipment, and not for a background check you have to pay a stranger for.

```message A "remote assistant" text
Channel: text
From: +1 (469) 555-0143
Hi! Your resume was recommended to us. We have a remote assistant role, 1-2 hours a day, $45/hour, no experience needed. Reply YES and our HR manager will add you on a messaging app to start today.
! You never sent them a resume
! High pay for little work and no experience
! Moves you to a chat app with no interview
```

## The ways a fake job takes your money

Once you reply, the "job" usually turns into one of these:

- **Pay for training or a starter kit.** You buy a $150 "certification" or a kit you never needed.
- **Equipment checks.** They send a check to buy a laptop from "our approved vendor." The check is fake, and the vendor is the scammer. (See the lesson on fake check scams.)
- **Task scams.** You "rate products" or "boost apps" and watch earnings grow on a dashboard. To unlock them, you have to deposit your own money first. You never get any of it back.
- **Reshipping.** You receive packages at home and mail them somewhere else. The items were bought with stolen cards, and you become part of the crime.
- **Moving money.** You receive payments and forward them. That makes you a money mule, which is illegal. (See the lesson on money mules.)

```message The task "job," one week in
Channel: dm
From: Mia | Talent Team (@brightpath.tasks)
Great work! Your account shows $612 in commissions. You hit a "premium order," so your balance is negative until you deposit $300 in crypto. Once you finish the set, you can withdraw everything.
! You have to pay to get paid
! Earnings only exist on their dashboard
! Asks for crypto, which is hard to get back
```

## A real job versus a scam

```table Real job or scam?
| | Real job | Scam |
| How you heard | You applied, or someone you know referred you | A stranger texted or DMed you first |
| Interview | In person or a video call with a real company | A quick chat in a messaging app, or none |
| Pay | Paid after you work, with a pay stub | Paid up front with a check, or "earnings" you can't withdraw |
| Money from you | Never | Training, kits, deposits, fees |
| Paperwork | W-4 and I-9 forms through the employer | Your bank login or a photo of your ID in a chat |
```

## Before you say yes

- Look up the company yourself. Check that its real website lists the job, and contact it through that site.
- Be wary of anyone who wants to move the conversation to a chat app right away.
- Never give a stranger your bank login, and do not share your Social Security number until you have a real offer from a company you have checked out. A real employer collects it through hiring paperwork, not a chat.
- Ask a parent, a teacher, or your school counselor to look at the offer with you.

You can report a fake job offer at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). The FTC describes job scams at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov).

```takeaways
- A real job pays you. If you have to pay first, it is a scam.
- Unasked-for job texts, chat-app interviews, and huge pay for easy work are red flags.
- Reshipping packages or forwarding money for a "job" can make you part of a crime.
```
