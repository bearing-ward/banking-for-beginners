Real investing builds wealth slowly, and the value of investments goes up and down. That is why the pitch "guaranteed 20% a week" is so tempting, and so false. No legitimate investment can promise big returns with no risk.

Investment scams often reach young people through social media: a stranger flexing cars and trading screenshots, a group chat with "signals," or a new online friend who happens to be great at crypto.

```message A "mentor" in your DMs
Channel: dm
From: @tradewithjayden_fx
Bro I turned $500 into $9k last month with my crypto system. Guaranteed 30% a week, zero risk. Spots in my VIP group close tonight. Send $250 in crypto to this wallet and I'll set you up.
! Guaranteed returns with "zero risk"
! A deadline to join
! Pay in crypto to a stranger's wallet
```

## Do the math on "guaranteed"

If an investment truly paid 30% a week, $100 would grow to more than $80 million in one year. Nothing pays like that. When a return sounds impossible, it is.

## The fake dashboard

Many crypto scams send you to a website or app that looks like a real trading platform. Your "balance" climbs fast. That number is not real. It is a picture designed to make you deposit more. When you try to withdraw, there is always another fee.

Sam, 18, deposited $500 after a DM like the one above. Within a week the site showed $2,000.

```ledger What the dashboard shows
Your deposit | $500.00
+ "Profits" shown on the screen | $1,500.00
= Balance on the dashboard | $2,000.00
# None of the profit exists.
```

```ledger What Sam actually lost
Original deposit | $500.00
+ "Withdrawal fee" to cash out | $300.00
+ "Tax" to unlock the account | $200.00
= Money sent to the scammer | $1,000.00
# Sam was never able to withdraw anything.
```

## When the "investor" is a new friend

Some scammers spend weeks building a friendship or romance by text before money comes up. Then they mention a crypto app that made them rich and offer to "teach" you. This is sometimes called a **romance-investment scam**. The friend, the app, and the profits are all part of the same scam.

A good rule: if someone you have only met online brings up investing, crypto, or money, stop and talk to someone you trust in real life.

## How to check an offer

- Anyone selling investments should be registered. You can look them up for free at [investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov).
- Guaranteed returns, pressure to act fast, and payment in crypto are three red flags.
- No real investment charges a fee to withdraw your own money that you have to pay in crypto or gift cards.

If you lost money, report it to the FBI at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov) and the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). Watch out for anyone who then offers to "recover" your crypto for a fee. That is a second scam.

```takeaways
- Real investments go up and down. Anyone who guarantees big returns is lying.
- A fake trading dashboard can show huge profits that do not exist.
- If an online friend brings up crypto or investing, treat it as a red flag.
- Check sellers at investor.gov, and report losses at ic3.gov.
```
