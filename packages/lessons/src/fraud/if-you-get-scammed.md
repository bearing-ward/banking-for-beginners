---
title: If you get scammed
level: starter
summary: Act fast, call the company you paid through, lock down your accounts, and report it. Getting scammed is not something to be ashamed of.
---

Scams work on everyone: teens, parents, teachers, even people who work in banks. If it happens to you, the most useful thing you can do is act quickly. The sooner you report it, the better your chance of stopping a payment or getting money back.

It is normal to feel embarrassed. Try not to let that slow you down. Telling a parent or another trusted adult right away is a smart move, not a confession.

## First, contact whoever moved the money

How you paid decides who to call first. Use the number on the back of your card, in the official app, or on the company's real website, never a number from the scammer.

```table Who to call first
| How you paid | Who to contact | What to ask |
| Debit or credit card | Your bank or card company | Dispute the charge and get a new card |
| Bank transfer or wire | Your bank | Try to reverse or recall the transfer |
| Payment app | The app, and the bank linked to it | Report fraud and try to stop the payment |
| Gift card | The company that issued the card | Ask whether the money can be frozen or refunded |
| Crypto | The crypto exchange you used | Report the scam; recovery is rare but report anyway |
```

## Then, lock things down

If the scammer got a password, a code, or personal details:

1. Change the password on that account, and on any other account where you used the same one.
2. Turn on two-factor authentication.
3. Sign out of all devices from the account's security settings.
4. If they got your Social Security number, go to [identitytheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov) and think about a credit freeze.

## Report it

Reporting helps investigators spot patterns and warn other people. It takes about 10 minutes.

- Report scams and fraud to the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).
- Report online scams, especially crypto and large losses, to the FBI at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov).
- Report the account to the app, marketplace, or social platform where it happened.
- For threats involving private images, see the lesson on sextortion scams.

Keep screenshots of messages, usernames, receipts, and any transaction IDs. You will need them.

## Watch out for the second scam

People who have been scammed are often targeted again by someone promising to get the money back, for a fee. Real government agencies and law enforcement do not charge you to recover money.

```message A "recovery" offer
Channel: email
From: case-desk@fund-recovery-bureau.org
Subject: Your lost funds have been located
We located the $1,200 you lost in the crypto investment scam. Our agents can return it within 48 hours after you pay a $250 release fee in Bitcoin. Act now; this case closes Friday.
! Knows you lost money and promises to get it back
! Charges a fee to recover your own money
! Wants crypto and a deadline
```

> [!tip] Make a note of what happened while it is fresh: dates, amounts, how you paid, and who you talked to. It makes every call and report faster.

```takeaways
- Act fast: contact the bank, card, or app you paid through first.
- Change passwords and turn on two-factor authentication if anything was shared.
- Report it at reportfraud.ftc.gov and, for online scams, ic3.gov.
- Anyone who charges a fee to "recover" your money is running a second scam.
```
