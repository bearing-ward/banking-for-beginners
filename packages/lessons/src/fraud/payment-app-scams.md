---
title: Payment app scams
level: starter
summary: Sending money on a payment app is like handing over cash. Learn the overpayment and "accidental send" tricks, and why to pay only people you know.
---

Payment apps such as Venmo, Zelle, and Cash App make it easy to split a pizza or pay a friend back for concert tickets. That speed is also why scammers love them. Once you send money, it usually lands in the other person's account in minutes, and you cannot pull it back.

Think of a **peer-to-peer (P2P) payment** as handing someone cash. If you paid the wrong person or got tricked, the app often cannot reverse it, because you approved the payment yourself.

> [!note] Some apps offer "purchase protection" only for payments marked as goods and services, and only for eligible purchases. Paying "friends and family" usually has no protection at all.

## The overpayment trick

Leo, 17, is selling his old gaming console online for $300. A buyer "pays" him $450 by mistake and asks for the extra $150 back. Leo sends it. A few days later, the $450 disappears: it came from a stolen card or a hacked account, and the payment was reversed.

```message The buyer's message
Channel: text
From: +1 (720) 555-0129
So sorry!! I typed $450 instead of $300. Can you send the $150 back to my other account @jay-pays-2? My mover is picking up the console today.
! Paid more than the price, then asks for a refund
! Wants the refund sent to a different account
! Rushes the pickup before the payment can fail
```

Leo lost the console and $150 of his own money.

```ledger What the overpayment cost Leo
"Payment" from the buyer | $450.00
- "Refund" Leo sent back | $150.00
- Original payment reversed | $450.00
= Change to Leo's balance | -$150.00
# Plus the $300 console the "mover" picked up.
```

## The "accidental send"

A stranger sends you $80 and then texts that it was a mistake. If you send $80 back, you might find the original payment was from a stolen account and gets reversed, so you end up paying twice. The safe move: do not send money back yourself. Contact the app's support through the app and let them reverse the original payment.

## Rules for payment apps

- Only pay people you know and trust in real life.
- Before you hit send, check the name, the photo, and the amount.
- When you buy from a stranger, use a payment method with protection, like a credit card or a marketplace's built-in checkout.
- Never "refund" an overpayment. Cancel the deal or ask the app to handle it.
- Turn on the app's PIN or fingerprint lock and alerts for every payment.

```table Who to send money to on a payment app
| Situation | Use the app? |
| Paying a friend back for lunch | Yes |
| Splitting rent with a roommate you know | Yes |
| Buying sneakers from a stranger online | No, use a protected method |
| A "buyer" asking for a refund of extra money | No, contact the app |
| A prize or job that needs a fee first | No, it is a scam |
```

If you already sent money to a scammer, report it to the app and your bank right away, then at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). The CFPB explains your rights with payment apps at [consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov).

```takeaways
- A payment app transfer is like cash: once you approve it, it is very hard to get back.
- Overpayment and "accidental send" stories end with you paying the scammer.
- Pay only people you know, and use protected payment methods with strangers.
- If money arrives by mistake, let the app reverse it. Do not send it back yourself.
```
