A **money mule** is someone who moves money for another person through their own bank account, payment app, or crypto wallet. The money almost always comes from crime: scams, stolen cards, or fraud against other people. Criminals need mules because their own accounts get shut down.

Teens and young adults are a favorite target. You have a clean bank account, you might need cash, and the request can sound harmless: "I can't get my money because my account is frozen. Can I send it to you and you send it to my cousin?"

```message A DM offering easy money
Channel: dm
From: @cashflip.daily
Want an easy $200 this week? I need to send some money through your Cash App or bank account. You keep $200 and send the rest where I tell you. 100% legit, my bank has a hold on me.
! Pays you for the use of your account
! Money in, money out, with a cut for you
! A story about why they can't use their own account
```

## It is still a crime if you were tricked

Moving stolen money is called **money laundering**, and it is a crime even if you did not know where the money came from. Saying "I was only helping" may not protect you. People who act as money mules can face:

- Their bank or payment app account being closed, which can make it hard to open a new one for years.
- Owing the bank all the money that moved through, if it was stolen.
- Damage to their credit.
- Criminal charges.

```ledger What the "easy $200" really costs
Money sent into your account | $2,000.00
- What you forward to "the boss" | $1,800.00
= Your "cut" | $200.00
- Bank takes back the stolen $2,000 | $2,000.00
= What you owe the bank | -$1,800.00
```

The person who recruited you keeps $1,800. You keep a debt of $1,800 and a closed account.

## How the asks are disguised

Money mule requests do not always say "move money for me." Watch for:

- A job where you receive payments and pass them on, often called "payment processing" or "funds transfer agent."
- An online friend or romantic interest who needs to "borrow" your account.
- Someone who wants your debit card, PIN, or login "for a day."
- Requests to open a new account in your name, or to buy crypto with money they send you.

```table Is this a money mule ask?
| Request | Mule? |
| Pay a friend back for half of a pizza | No |
| Receive $1,500 from a stranger and send it on in gift cards | Yes |
| Let a "friend" log in to your bank app to "test" it | Yes |
| A "job" that pays you to accept and forward transfers | Yes |
```

## What to do

Say no, stop replying, and block the sender. Never share your bank login, card, or PIN. If you already moved money for someone, stop now, tell a trusted adult, and call your bank. It is better to speak up early. You can report it at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) or to the FBI at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov).

```takeaways
- If you are already involved, stop, tell a trusted adult, and call your bank.
```
