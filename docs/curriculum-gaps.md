# Curriculum gaps

Reviewed November 14, 2026. The catalog contains 86 lessons across 12 topics, with no empty planned topics or content-log warnings. This is a targeted coverage review of the catalog and relevant lessons, not a factual recertification of all 86 lessons. No content review dates were advanced.

The [CEE and Jump$tart national standards](https://www.jumpstart.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2021_Natl_Standards_Downloadable_final.pdf) provide a useful coverage reference. The recommendations below are editorial judgments based on the current lessons.

## Implemented November 14, 2026

The curriculum now has 93 lessons. Seven additions and three expanded lessons address the findings below:

- `budgeting/when-money-runs-short` shows a projected negative balance, a confirmed payment arrangement, and a revised weekly plan.
- `budgeting/comparing-prices` covers unit prices, discounts, hypothetical tax, fees, cash limits, and waste.
- `credit/debt-collectors` covers verification, written disputes, records, and separate court deadlines.
- `saving/inflation-and-buying-power` compares hypothetical savings growth with a changing grocery basket.
- `work/reading-a-pay-stub` adds a missing-hours example, a correction request, and labor-agency escalation. It also distinguishes withholding from final tax liability.
- `work/first-paycheck-challenge`, `budgeting/apartment-plan-challenge`, and `budgeting/unexpected-bill-challenge` ask for a decision before showing worked answers and action quizzes.
- `turning-18/reading-a-contract` adds a cosigner's repayment shortfall.
- `college/cost-of-attendance` compares complete aid offers and distinguishes grants, borrowing, and wages earned later.

Sources are linked in the lessons. The original findings follow for context; they are no longer an open backlog. These additions do not recertify unchanged lessons.

## Address first

### When your paycheck does not cover your bills

`budgeting/making-a-budget` suggests a low realistic income month. `budgeting/paying-bills-on-time` already covers a bill calendar, buffers, changing due dates, and contacting a company before a missed payment. What is missing is a worked recovery plan when those measures are insufficient.

Add a lesson after paying bills on time. Give a teen fewer shifts than expected, several bills, and insufficient cash. Have the learner protect essentials, stop optional spending, ask about hardship arrangements, and revise a weekly running balance. Include how to find local nonprofit assistance. Avoid implying that every shortfall can be solved by cutting wants or saving more.

Use the [CFPB cash-flow budget](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/documents/10038/cfpb_creating-cash-flow-budget_tool_2021-08.pdf) as a starting reference. Success means the learner can spot a negative balance before a due date and explain a feasible response.

### What to do about a debt collector

`credit/credit-reports` mentions collections, and `credit/buy-now-pay-later` mentions missed payments reaching collectors. Neither teaches the response to a collection notice.

Add a credit lesson about verifying the collector and debt, keeping records, finding the applicable dispute process, and getting help with a court notice. Include a fictional notice and a choice between verifying it through an independent channel and paying an unsolicited caller. Check current rules before teaching deadlines or legal consequences.

Use the [FTC debt collection FAQs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/debt-collection-faqs). Success means the learner knows what to verify before sharing information or paying, and knows not to ignore court papers.

### Comparing everyday prices

`money-basics/opportunity-cost` and `money-basics/hours-of-work` teach trade-offs. The curriculum lacks a focused exercise comparing unit prices, discounts, delivery fees, sales tax, and the final checkout total.

Add a budgeting lesson using fictional grocery sizes and two purchase options. Make the larger package cheaper per unit but unaffordable within the learner's cash limit, or likely to go unused. Success means the learner calculates comparable prices and explains why the lowest unit price is not always the best purchase.

## Address next

### Inflation and buying power

The saving and investing lessons discuss nominal balances and returns without an explicit inflation example. Add a short saving lesson that compares the same basket of goods over time and distinguishes a growing account balance from growing buying power. Use hypothetical rates rather than unsourced forecasts. Keep the explanation clear that deposit insurance does not guarantee purchasing power.

### Fixing an incorrect paycheck

`work/minimum-wage-and-teen-work-rules` already recommends recording hours and contacting the Wage and Hour Division. Extend that into a worked mismatch between a time record and a pay stub, a message requesting correction, and an escalation path using current Department of Labor guidance. This could be an expansion of `work/reading-a-pay-stub` rather than another lesson.

### Putting several skills together

Individual lessons have quizzes and calculators, but the curriculum would benefit from a few scenarios that combine them. Start with a first paycheck, a first apartment, and an unexpected bill. Ask the learner to reconcile a statement, build a feasible plan, identify a risk, and explain the next action. Each scenario needs explicit expected answers and checked arithmetic.

## Existing topics to deepen before adding duplicates

- Cosigning already appears in `turning-18/reading-a-contract`, teen credit, and private student loans. Add a consequence example rather than another definition. The [FTC cosigning FAQs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/cosigning-loan-faqs) offer a source for the risks.
- Comparing college aid offers already appears in `college/cost-of-attendance`. A side-by-side worked offer would deepen it without repeating FAFSA basics.
- Bill calendars and irregular expenses already have coverage. A new cash-flow lesson should focus on weekly balances and recovery rather than repeating those definitions.

Before publishing any addition, follow `authoring-lessons.md`, add the slug in reading order, run `lessons:show`, record the source change with `content:log`, and run verification. Legal and yearly financial figures need current official sources at authoring time.
